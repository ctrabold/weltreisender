## Wednesday, 12 May 2010

## Electronics in Everyday Travel

Too heavy and not needed: the GPS, the mobile phone and all their chargers stay at home!

All I've brought along is my iPod Touch and the little Shuffle - and that decision has really paid off!

Both pack down small and can be charged from any computer via USB, no special charger required.

### iPod Touch - a good, flexible travel companion (if a bit slow at times)

So far the iPod Touch has survived everything with flying colours. Desert sand, heat and sweat - no problem at all!

What annoys me about the iPod Touch:

- short battery life
- it gets sluggish, and syncing with iTunes takes forever. The music library is out of action for up to 20 minutes.

  That happens even if you only add a single new track. Very irritating.
- The constant password prompts for the iTunes Store drive me up the wall, too. They even pop up several times while you're downloading an album, and then interrupt the download.

What's brilliant about the iPod Touch:

- depending on your mood, you can add new apps and music over Wi-Fi
- browsing the web and writing emails is easy
- reading e-books
- playing games, or otherwise making (better) use of all the waiting around

### iPod Shuffle

On the bike in particular I've come to really appreciate the little one, and often wore it clipped to the sleeve of my cycling jersey.

The earphones that come with it are fine for cycling. Unlike in-ear headphones, they don't block out the surrounding noise, so you still hear enough of the outside world to move safely through traffic. Wind noise while riding is minimal, but sadly they offer next to no "horn protection".

Unfortunately it takes ages for the battery to charge fully.

### Headphones - the single point of failure

Even though I packed them carefully in a watertight Lock & Lock box, both pairs of headphones have given up.

The expensive Sennheiser headphones developed a loose connection at the plug and have been out of action since Thailand. Apart from that, they give excellent sound and superb protection against horns and snoring. Luckily, a fellow traveller gave me a spare pair.

Important with in-ear headphones: make sure you have spare ear tips! They get lost in no time and apparently can't be bought on their own. At least I couldn't find them sold separately anywhere in South East Asia.

Since China I haven't been able to use the iPod Shuffle at all, because the control on the headphone cable is broken, so I can't change the volume or skip tracks.

### And next time?

I'd pack exactly the same way again!

I never really missed a GPS. It can be interesting to know how much climbing you've done, but to my mind that doesn't justify the extra weight. As a navigation aid, a GPS device would never have paid off, because decent maps are often missing anyway. A good compass is far more useful and does the job perfectly well!

A mobile phone would at most have been handy in big cities for arranging where to meet, if you had a date. Out in the countryside it's useless.

A charger that can run on solar and/or dynamo power would be really useful. One option I'm going to look into more closely can be found at <http://zzing.de/>, for example.
