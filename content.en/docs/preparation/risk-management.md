## Risk Management and Questions About Planning the Trip

Before you set off, all sorts of scenarios play out in your head. Friends, acquaintances and my own family bombard me with questions.

All of it full of worry, all of it full of love. Even so - or rather, precisely because of that - I quickly learn to ignore their questions and keep my feet firmly on the ground.

So I use a trick that has served me well in everyday project work: I write a list of the worst things that could happen to me.

I also define my own goals and conditions. Surprisingly, these turn out to be the answers to the most common questions too.

These are the questions meant to calm my doubts:

### Do I know what I'm letting myself in for?

Yes and no. I know it may get tough and that I'll find out where my real limits are. But I'm aware of the risk and I'm happy to take it for the experience.

### Do I see it as a once-in-a-lifetime chance?

Sort of. Although I find that a bit silly. Can't I decide where my life is heading at any other time, too?

### Do I have the time and money?

Over these months I'm saving 15,000 EUR.

### Do I know how long I'm planning to travel for?

No, not yet. On the one hand I'd like to travel without any time pressure; on the other, I'm afraid I'll never want to come back and will lose my sense of home. Can I allow myself to get lost in the journey? Can I picture, from where I stand now, the goal I'll be pedalling towards the whole time?

### What do my dream destination and dream route look like?

Away from here. But is it running away? No, it's curiosity that drives me - discovering something new, seeing a different world and maybe even getting to know it. My dream destinations are New Zealand, the Philippines, maybe Thailand, India, Japan, South America, feeling the wind in my face at Cape Horn, breathing in the scent of the pine forests in Canada. My dream route would be to just keep riding to the right, sometimes more uphill and downhill, sometimes less, and then roll back into Dieburg, Germany, from the left...

### Have I got (hold of) enough information to get my bearings?

Yes. Sources:

- ADFC (the German cyclists' association)
- Maps
- The internet

### Am I travelling alone or with company?

I'm going alone.

### Which means of transport have I chosen?

My bike, boat, train.

I hope I've made a good choice with the bike. It still feels very stiff and awkward. I'll fall back on boats and trains in an emergency. Planes I'd avoid whenever possible.

I'm drawing up a checklist where I can tick off or cross out everything. That gives me a sense of security and clarity, which matters a lot, especially in the days before departure.

## Risk List

These are the risks I identified before my trip, so I could think through possible solutions in advance.

- I don't get a visa
- I fall seriously ill
- I fall ill
- I get saddle sore
- I get a puncture
- I can't be bothered any more
- I've forgotten X or Y
- I have to get home quickly
- I never want to go back
- I die of boredom
- I get lost
- I forget something and notice too late
- I fall in love
- I lose one or more panniers
- I lose my money
- I lose my return ticket
- I lose my papers
- I don't understand a thing
- I get kidnapped
- I get arrested and have to go to prison
- I get beaten up
- I get mugged
- My bike falls apart completely
- My tools give up the ghost
- My credit card snaps / stops working / gets stolen / gets lost
- I run out of snack bars
- I run out of loo roll
- I run out of water
- I'm refused entry to a country
- I run out of food

Having the answers helps me stay calmer and enjoy the trip more.
