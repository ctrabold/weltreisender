## Sunday, 29 November 2009

The Great Feast - Guangzhou, Show Me Your Kitchens!

​27 to 29 November

{{< gallery from="2009-11-27" to="2009-11-29" >}}

The city is frantic and wound up - above all, the air feels much dirtier than in any city so far. You can feel it straight away; it presses down, steams and wraps itself around you. Like an underground car park in midsummer.

There's plenty going on for the ears too: once again people honk for all they're worth, and lots of building sites make a racket (even at night). Your choice: "give as good as you get" or "put your earplugs in".

My short (several-hour) trip from the riverbank through the city takes me through the main shopping street in the centre. Compared with this, Schildergasse in Cologne, Germany - even on the very, very last Saturday before Christmas - is a JOKE. I've never seen anything like it and I'm glad when I'm out again.

The metro is painfully cramped - especially compared directly with Hong Kong - and buying a ticket is baffling and long-winded. Why keep it simple when you can make it complicated...

All in all, everything is gloriously chaotic, and with a pinch of humour and composure I can cope with it for a few days. But woe betide me if my stomach starts rumbling! So I spend the rest of my time exploring culinary delights:

On Friday evening it's seafood. The restaurant is right on the bank of the Pearl River and offers a good glimpse of the nightlife.

You can put your dishes together however you like. You walk past big water tanks and cages with the waitress and point at the "contents" you want. Everything from mussels and sea snakes to larger birds is on offer. As a snack you can have spicy pickled scorpions or half snakes.

On Saturday we find a German restaurant not far from the river. Over a good stein of beer and hearty food, I think back to the moments in the desert when Noel and I longed for exactly this. And now here I am, stein in hand, eating Fleischpflanzerl (Bavarian meatballs) and Wiener Schnitzel. Unbelievable.

On Sunday I get another lesson and enjoy a lunch of Cantonese delicacies - a dream! By comparison, the much-praised - because so varied - food of Sichuan ([http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine)) seems rather crude to me.

To avoid any misunderstanding: I really like the food in Sichuan! But there the chilli heat usually dominates. With the local Cantonese dishes, for example, I couldn't even tell where the heat was coming from. It's even more of a game with your taste buds. A dance on the tongue.

The farewell meal: a little sushi snack.

But that's it for the gluttony!

Tomorrow it's back to Emeishan and then over the mountains to Kunming. I'm expecting limited supplies and an extra-tough route profile. The few extra pounds I've put on here should melt away again then.

Another advantage of a cycling trip: you can overindulge without a shred of guilt, as long as you burn the calories off on the road afterwards.
