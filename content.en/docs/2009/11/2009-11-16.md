## Monday, 16 November 2009

The Leaning Tower of Visas

Chengdu, 16 November

At 10 o'clock I set off in the pouring rain for the PSB, the Public Security Bureau, to extend my visa. It's thoroughly miserable out, the streets riddled with puddles. A stiff wind sweeps through the alleys.

I take the bus into the city centre, find the building straight away, hop up to the second floor and take a ticket.

After a few minutes it's my turn. I go to the counter, hand over my documents and explain why I need the extension. This time they want the following:

1. A current bank statement or some other proof that I have enough money. A copy of my Maestro and Visa cards seems to do the trick, though.
2. A copy of the photo page and the visa page in my passport
3. A copy of the hotel receipt
4. A visa application (type L, maximum 30 days, 0 entries, expiry date 30 days from today!)

I'm told to make the copies on the first floor (= ground floor). When I get there, I find out the photocopiers are broken. But there's a copy shop across the street.

I join the queue there and then hurry back to the counter. This time a different official waves me over.

It's all going rather smoothly today, I think to myself - just as the clerk, leafing through my passport, gives me to understand that I will NOT get an extension for my visa. I have to leave the country within three days.

There's no point trying to explain that that's impossible by bike. She gets up and disappears for lunch. No mercy, no extension.

The reason for her decision: back in Delingha I had my type F visa (= business visa) converted into an L visa (= tourist visa). And this L visa can NOT be extended like a normal L visa. I'm also baffled as to why the visa agency gave me an F visa in the first place. Probably because I wanted a multiple-entry visa back then for my planned visit to Hong Kong - which, as an EU citizen, you don't even need for Hong Kong!! I'm fuming that I paid good money for this "service".

So here's my tip: take your time over visa matters! Noel, for example, applied for his Chinese visa directly (with a short note explaining it was a cycling trip) and got back an L visa for 90 days!

A bit crestfallen, I head back to the hostel. Even though I have a plan B up my sleeve: it does hurt a little to _have to_ leave China like this, because right now I feel really at home here.

Over lunch I decide to make a little detour to Hong Kong, apply for a new visa there and then come back here. Nothing else really makes sense. All the sources I've consulted for this decision give me hope that the mission will succeed! Keep your fingers crossed for me all the same!

Yes, the "cycle to Singapore" mission has wobbled a bit. "What if I don't get a new visa?" and so on... but I have a plan B for that too. I've got all the important things with me; if push comes to shove, I'll just become a backpacker... :-/

So _don't worry_!
