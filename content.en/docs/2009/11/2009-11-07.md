## Saturday, 7 November 2009

Pandamania

Chengdu, 4 to 7 November

The alarm goes off at 7 - hooray, panda time! I'm off on a trip to the panda breeding centre in Chengdu!!

{{< gallery from="2009-11-04" to="2009-11-07" >}}

For the first time on my trip I'm signing up for one of those tourist "excursion packages": almost every hotel or hostel organises trips to various sights in the surrounding area.

I normally loathe this kind of outing, because a) I'll pass these sights on my bike anyway, or b) these "sights" don't interest me because they're overrun.

This time, though, I climb down - quite literally - from my high horse (bike) and let myself in for what looks like a tourist trap. Besides, Rafael (Valencia, Spain) and Elay (Israel) are coming along too - two likeable roommates from the youth hostel.

The package costs around 5 EUR and includes the bus transfer and my very own PandaCard, which seems pretty fair to me. Once the PandaCard has been officially registered (passport, phone call to the panda consulate), we get the OK and our bus seats are reserved.

To be honest, the chubby little things put me off more than they interest me.

Lazy, fat and boring - roughly speaking, that was my image of pandas until this morning.

HOWEVER: the visit, and the whole organisation, was great, pleasantly relaxed and above all VERY FUNNY!

We arrive at the enclosure just after 8. It's quiet, very green and surprisingly clean.

As we arrive it's feeding time, and the first panda is chasing an apple that a keeper is waving back and forth above its head. Wow! Not lazy at all.

The other bears are hugely active too, scrapping like little rascals in the undergrowth, sneakily shoving each other off the swing and clambering cheekily up the trees - only to come crashing down again shortly afterwards with a loud thump.

I often get the feeling that we're watching little children in panda costumes putting on a show for us. It's priceless!

The tourist crowds are manageable. What takes some getting used to is the "OK let's go!" with which we're steered, politely but firmly, to the next enclosure. As we leave the park, we see the first LOUD Chinese tour group - complete with flag bearer and megaphone. Lucky escape!

Around midday we get back to the hostel in high spirits, and in the evening we enjoy the delicious BBQ, where I meet Jessica from Leipzig, Germany. She also spent a long time cycling around the world and is visiting China this time for her biology project. Thanks for a great evening, and good luck with the project!

The next day I plan the next leg of my journey and have a long chat with Peter about China and the region.

The day after, I leave Chengdu with mixed feelings. There's surely still sooo much to discover in the city, but at the same time the countryside around it is calling me.

Once again it's clear to me: you could spend years in China if you wanted to. There's so much to see! But I have to move on, because my visa is (once again) about to run out. I'll try to extend it again in Kunming.
