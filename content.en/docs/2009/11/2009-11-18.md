## Wednesday, 18 November 2009

Hong Kong!

I get out of bed without any trouble and spend 8:20 to 9:20 on a thrilling taxi ride to the airport. Right on time, at half past ten, the plane takes off. I'm the only Westerner on board.

The flight is very pleasant. There's good food and, just before landing, a little dance performance by the flight attendants. In the background the Shenzhen Airlines promo video plays with tinkly music, while the ladies sway their hips and hands with Asian finesse in the narrow aisle. Very different, but rather charming. I try to imagine that in Germany. With oompah music and dirndls. :)

After a short two hours in the air, the first factory buildings of Shenzhen emerge beneath the thick blanket of fog. And whoosh, I'm already on the bus to Shenzhen station. There I follow the crowds and reach the Chinese border.

The trickiest thing today is getting hold of the "Health Declaration Form On Entry/Exit", which you have to fill in because of swine flu and so on. Even though everyone here is running around with one of these forms in their hand, the woman at the counter just won't understand me... Where do I get this form? After some back and forth the penny drops and she digs out two forms from under her counter.

COMPLETELY different a few metres further on in Hong Kong: the forms are laid out for you to help yourself, and there's a counter with a proper sign. (Yes, you have to fill in this form both when leaving China and when entering Hong Kong. The forms differ slightly in layout and the details they ask for.)

At passport control I hand in my departure card and get my Chinese exit stamp. "Bye, bye China!", I hum - and then, out of the blue, there's one more security check on the Chinese side!

I have to open my little bag (handlebar bag), and the official's hand lands on the Lonely Planet "China". He inspects the book with a critical eye and my stomach tightens: I seem to remember some China travellers at the youth hostel in Bishkek talking about the book and the border guards. There was something...

The official explains: on the back of the book there's a small map of China and its neighbours. Chinese territory is highlighted in orange. Taiwan is NOT highlighted, which is why the map - and therefore the book - may not be brought into China.

I point out that I'm on my way out, and with a faint smile the official lets me know I should watch out when I come back in, or the border guards will confiscate the book!

After that brief moment of panic comes the final passport control on the Hong Kong side - and the way to the train station is already in sight!

A bit of typing on the computer and bang! there's the stamp for a 90-day stay in my passport. 90 days!! The world can be so simple! I'm happy!

Then onwards, following the stream of people, to the station. At the friendly ticket counter I buy a ticket into the city and get into an empty carriage. Unbelievable: there are even "Quiet areas" here, and the "Mind the gap!" familiar from London. I feel completely at ease. Everyone speaks English, signs are bilingual Chinese-English, the train is clean and quiet.

A little later the train rolls through the hilly landscape into the city. During the journey I research where I can extend my visa, and find a travel agency practically next to a metro station.

Shortly before six o'clock I sign the visa application. I can collect the visa on Friday afternoon: 90 days, 1 entry (two if you like), valid from 19.11.2009, for just under 60 EUR. Is it Christmas already? That was easy for once! Hopefully everything is "China-compliant" too.

Round the corner from the travel agency I spot a "Guesthouse" sign. At 30 EUR it's unusually expensive, but I suppose that's OK for Hong Kong. The street is fairly quiet and not as crowded as the others.

On my short detour (300 metres) along Nathan Road I must have been approached 20 times: watches, hash, "cheaper accommodation" and so on.

In my own room I can hopefully catch my breath for the first time today and sort out my thoughts! It's been an insane day. Thanks for keeping your fingers crossed!
