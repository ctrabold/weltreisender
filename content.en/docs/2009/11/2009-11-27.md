## Friday, 27 November 2009

I'm in Guangzhou - and where are Dou?

​26 to 27 November

I round off my Hong Kong visit with a little trek through the "New Territories". In the woods at Bride's Pool I enjoy the pretty waterfall and the peace and quiet.

![](/images/2009/11/26/4139748903.jpg)

Then, refreshed and rested, it's back to noisy China.

The border crossing is no problem. The stickers on the Lonely Planet cover stay put all the same (I'd covered Taiwan with a load of price stickers).

On the other side there's an immediate warm welcome. Before I reach the bus station I have to tear myself away from five women breathing "Maasaasch?" at me (hostesses) - the massage service is clearly a double entendre. Luckily I quickly find the bus to Guangzhou and can finally stretch my legs. The bus TV shows a mixture of karaoke to Chinese folk music and nature footage.

While leopards tear gazelles apart, the Chinese singer croons something in Chinese, with the lyrics to sing along to at the bottom of the screen. Fortunately none of the passengers takes them up on it, and we hum quietly along the motorway.

For most of the journey I daydream about Hong Kong. It was great, I saw a lot, and I now have a completely new picture of Hong Kong. I don't regret the short trip in the slightest; I'm heading back with a very good feeling.

In Guangzhou I'm flying completely blind at first: the bus spits me out at a huge bus station, and I've arrived somewhere in the city.

It's pitch dark. I look in vain for signs or a service desk. There's also nobody I could - or would want to - ask. All around me, dark figures are streaming either somewhere else or straight towards me.

So first I have to battle my way through a crowd of moped and taxi drivers trying to talk me into a ride to "the best hotel in town". I find some light under a street lamp and look for my current position on the map. In vain! There are several bus stations, so for now I keep going in the same direction and trust to luck - I definitely don't want to go back to the rip-off merchants, and my big-city instinct tells me I'm on the right track.

I carry on through the dark alleys, find a phone in a kiosk and call Daniel. He tells the taxi driver where I need to go.

Then everything happens very fast: taxi ride, hug Daniel, check into the hotel, eat sushi, watch Stromberg (a German TV comedy). At some point I fall into bed, stuffed full of new impressions and _Cuba Libre_.

For tomorrow I'm planning some gentle sightseeing and a visit to the banks of the Pearl River.
