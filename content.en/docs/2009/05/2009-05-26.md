## Tuesday, 26 May 2009

## To Bayreuth with a pulse of 200 (nearly)

![](/images/2009/05/26/3572238117.jpg)

After a quick chat with my camping neighbour about where my bike trip starts and ends, at around 9 o'clock I pedal wistfully past the thermal baths one more time. I soon realise: today won't be a walk in the park. After just five kilometres there's no trace of relaxation left. It's up and down, left and right, like a rollercoaster.

On the descents I often have to brake hard because the signpost only comes into view too late. Later I just trust that the route keeps going straight on and carry as much momentum as possible into the next hill. It's amazing, though, how quickly the energy fizzles out. On every (!) climb I have to shift down into first gear and then crawl up to the top at 5-6 km/h. It almost gives me blisters on my shifting thumb, and my pulse climbs to around 160 bpm. That's way outside my aerobic zone = more hunger!!

Unlike the previous stretch, though, there's not a beer garden in sight - just plenty of lorries and motorway bridges instead. So I pedal through the countryside in a slightly sour mood. Luckily the traffic eases off later, but the first chance to eat comes almost just before Bayreuth. There the "Moreth" inn invites me to linger. I wol… savour two big plates of potato salad and two half-litres of shandy.

Three more climbs later, I still reach Bayreuth at just before 6 pm, completely drained. The town seems to be full of signs and signposts. Traffic squeezes its way around the centre. On instinct I ride to the fountain in front of the opera house, where I catch my breath for a moment and let a local (and fellow passionate traveller) refresh my memory of Bayreuth's history.

He raves about Prague, Kraków, Ukraine and Russia and gives me fresh strength. My head is clear again and I even make it up to Wagner's Festival Theatre, where I take a spontaneous siesta on a park bench.

It's just after 6 pm and I still haven't got anywhere to stay. Bayreuth apparently has room (only) for the fine arts, a golf course and a spa... I haven't seen any hotels in town.

After a few ups and downs I find a place to stay at the Grunau Hotel just outside Bayreuth - even with free Wi-Fi, which is why I can finally write all this to you.

From here on I'm leaving the well-sheltered Main cycle path and entering the wide world of the EuroVelo cycle networks. If all goes well, I'll soon be riding on Czech soil. _Butterflies in my stomach_
