## Tuesday, 12 May 2009

## The "Chains, Spokes, Valves" Workshop

![](/images/2009/05/11/4617194390.jpg)

I've spent the last two days in the workshop, taking my bike completely apart and putting it back together again.

Under the expert eye of my trusted local bike mechanic, I got to know every single bolt on first-name terms, checking and tightening each one. I also bled the brakes, fitted new brake pads, swapped the chain and put on new tyres.

Tyres are a notorious weak spot, so I didn't cut any corners: new plastic rim tape and extra-tough touring tyres. I also learnt how to re-spoke a wheel, in case one or more spokes snap.

So from now on I can handle the servicing myself with a clear conscience.

A nice bonus: I got to put my tools to the test straight away.

The "Alien II" multi-tool was cast aside within minutes - it turned out to be _completely_ useless for my purposes. Too heavy, too clunky. And 95% of its gadgets would be no use to me anyway (keyword: ​*feature bloat*​)! I don't need any of the other "all-in-one tools" either: six Allen keys and a Torx key are all it takes to fix this bike!

The alternative to a multi-tool is a handful of small, dedicated Allen and Torx keys that weigh next to nothing and feel great in the hand.

### A quick postscript

My advice to anyone planning a trip: before you buy any tools, take a good look at your bike. Which bolts does it have? What sizes are they? Then take that list to the shop and buy only the tools you really need. Everything else is dead weight!
