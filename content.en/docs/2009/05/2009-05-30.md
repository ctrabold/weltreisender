## Saturday, 30 May 2009

Chilling out in Karlsbad

Every Friday and Saturday the "Egerländer" holds a hearty folk dance night, which is very well attended. The place itself is "hearty" from top to bottom, too, kitted out with beer steins, old pictures and all sorts of Bavarian/Egerland knick-knacks.

I'm staying in the "Axel" room - one floor below is the dance floor, where young and old alike strut their stuff. My room is bathed alternately in red, then blue light by the disco lights. It's all very authentic and the musician is really good (he gets every hit going out of that keyboard! Sensational!), but restful sleep is sadly out of the question. So I seize the moment and use the light effects for my own party. And so I celebrate late into the night to old hits for the grannies, 80s tunes and so on.

Despite the short night, I start the new day fresh and rested. For my rest day I put on my hiking boots and explore the Karlsbad countryside. Slowly I'm getting a clearer picture of KV and discover beautiful corners and views. A few metres from my hotel, the famous Karlsbad thermal water shoots up out of the ground as a fountain. And the weather is much milder today.

At 4 pm "Ave Maria" is performed in the very pretty church of "St Mary Magdalene".

The already moving singing reaches its climax when the Ave Maria suddenly rings out in German (Schubert's version?). The feeling that's been creeping up on me since yesterday becomes clearer again: now I'm a foreigner, now a new stage of the journey begins. Signs are getting harder to read, conversations or small talk with the people around me are becoming rare, or rather non-existent. How lucky that I know a bit of Russian: lots of signs are in Cyrillic script, and some Czechs (still) understand a bit of руссй. That gives me some security.

As I leave the church, big fat drops are falling and an icy wind is blowing through the valley. Tomorrow I'm riding on to Plzeň - come what may!
