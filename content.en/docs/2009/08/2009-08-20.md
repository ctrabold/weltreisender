## Thursday, 20 August 2009

## On to Naryn - or Maybe Not

The night is noisy and I can hardly sleep. Every hour, Arabic-oriental chanting rings out nearby. Preparations for the approaching Ramadan?

Again and again I picture myself collecting my parcel from the post office later. In between, anxious thoughts that it's "gone missing". The two authors from last night strongly advised me to send important post only by DHL. You (sadly) can't trust the normal post. I'm getting a bit nervous, but I keep believing it'll all work out.

Parcel number 2 has been on its way for almost two weeks and should bring me an important tool for tensioning my chain, plus spare bolts and maps for the countries ahead.

Please, please let it arrive today!

When the sun is in a good position, I hurry off to the post office. A few days ago, after a long search, I'd found the right counter - but the parcel wasn't there yet.

> **After a few minutes I'm holding the parcel in my hands! It's here! THANK YOU!**

![](/images/2009/08/20/3838942163.jpg)

Excited, I ride back to the hostel (the bumpy road even knocks my chain off on the way), pack up Rondolf and head to the nearest park, where I can finally tension my chain in peace and stow away the maps.

![](/images/2009/08/20/3838942165.jpg)

Overjoyed, I can now pedal with gusto again! :)

At a crossroads a young Dutchman speaks to me and asks whether I'm new in town and getting on all right. I ask him the same, and we get talking.

Afterwards we stroll through the streets and I show him the restaurant, the tourist info, the map shop, DHL and so on.

Shortly before 3 pm I decide to stay another night in Bishkek and follow him to his hostel.

The hostel is obviously well known, well kept and friendly. Especially compared with the dump I'd spent the previous nights in (for the same money).

In the garden we eat watermelon and chat about our travels. Later a Frenchman joins us. Our plans are almost identical. But he's still waiting for his Chinese visa, so I'll definitely be carrying on alone.

The guestbook makes exciting reading. Lots of people from all sorts of countries. A young German woman arrived yesterday too.

Let's see what the evening brings. If all goes well, I'll set off towards Naryn tomorrow.

Warm greetings from muggy Bishkek!
