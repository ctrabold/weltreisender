## Saturday, 1 August 2009

## The Free Melon Market Economy

![](/images/2009/08/01/3833261284.jpg)

My hopes of a quiet night are sawn to pieces shortly after three by my roommate's gentle but relentless snoring.

Still, I keep my cool, wake up fresh the next morning and set off along the well-built motorway towards Volgodonsk.

The ride is unspectacular, looooong and flat. The only bit of variety is an unexpected visit to a (melon) market. After hours of pedalling along a quiet country road, the road suddenly turns into a bustling market. I slow down and become the star attraction. I can feel all eyes following me as I roll slowly and contentedly through the narrow lanes.

To be honest, after such a dull ride I actually enjoy the feeling and have real fun playing up my unusual appearance. Especially as the reactions are so positive and warm. Besides peppers and kvass, the market sells one thing above all: watermelons. Everywhere! At every stall the pale green balls are piled metres high.

Even the oldest Lada seems to have room in the boot for a few melons, because every few kilometres there's a car at the roadside selling them.

For weight reasons I've only been tempted into buying one honeydew melon so far.

As darkness falls, there's still no gostinitsa (guesthouse) in sight. Quite the opposite: I'm on an embankment along the Don, surrounded by tall reeds.

![](/images/2009/08/01/3832499127.jpg)

The road runs some distance away to my left, with the odd clump of trees in between. The river is right next to me on the right. Every now and then there's a roadside market or a group of people bathing in the river. Otherwise nothing happens.

It's still more than 20 kilometres to Volgodonsk. I won't make it today. So I look for a cheap spot to pitch my tent on the riverbank.

There are still too many people about to put up my tent. After almost 110 kilometres and 6:30 hours in the saddle, I'm in no mood for a "Hello my German friend" interview. I just want to stretch my legs in peace and sleep.

When a lock blocks the path, a dog comes charging towards me, barking loudly. And a man.

I gesture that I need to keep going, which he advises against. The path doesn't go any further here. But if I like, I can pitch my tent here.

No sooner said than done, and I fall asleep by the rushing stream the moment my tent is up.
