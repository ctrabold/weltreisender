## Wednesday, 19 August 2009

Half a Kilo of Palmiers, Please, and Make It Quick!

The feeling hit hard and out of nowhere: as I step out of the internet café onto the street, I feel helpless and suddenly completely overwhelmed.

I know this situation (luckily) from the stories and books of other touring cyclists I read before my trip: the ground opens up and all you want is to sink into it. A nasty mix of homesickness and pure stress sweeps through your brain.

The thoughts are whipped up by the street noise, thousands of new smells and the stares of complete strangers. For the first time I feel a bit sick; I spent the night in bed with stomach cramps.

On top of that, I've no idea where I'm going to sleep tonight, and the pain of saying goodbye to the Welsh couple is only slowly fading. Utter helplessness, sooo far from home. I'm on the verge of full-blown panic.

If it weren't for that little flicker of hope, and knowing in advance that this situation would happen at some point during my trip (and again every now and then), I probably would have panicked.

Instead, I look for fixed points, familiar places in this new world. Nearby there's a little grocery shop where we bought our evening beer yesterday.

There I buy a Baltika 0% (alcohol-free beer), half a kilo of palmiers (pig's-ear biscuits) and some other sweet biscuits. Then calm my blood sugar for a moment and settle down. Drink water. Breathe. Carry on.

Shortly afterwards I get back on the bike, roll down to the main road, turn into the park and, with the help of two friendly policemen, find a cheap hostel nearby.

I spend the evening and the whole of the next day there in bed, reading and listening to music. I have a slight temperature and I'm glad to have a loo nearby.

The loo situation, however, is very odd. On my floor there are toilets and washbasins that apparently only open in the evening - which very nearly proves my undoing when I want to go about my morning routine.

Everything stays within the green zone. It's nothing serious either. Not _proper_ diarrhoea, just a slightly upset stomach, which I put down to the (large amounts of) delicious! greasy food and the heat. Maybe the mysterious hot paste I was given with my noodle soup for breakfast in the valley yesterday is to blame, because Lesley and Ben are absolutely fine. They gave that experiment a miss.

I dutifully drink rehydration salts and, at long last, feel like I'm getting some restful sleep.
