## Wednesday, 19 August 2009

## Unexpected Route Planning in the Middle of the Night

Late at night, on my way to the loo at the hostel, I bump into a young woman. She's from St Petersburg and speaks very good German.

She and her travel partner from Augsburg, Germany, are working on the "Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan" guidebook for the [Reise Know-How publishing house](http://www.reise-know-how.de/) and only today found out everything there is to know about the "Torugart Pass".

What a wonderful coincidence! I can hardly believe it. There isn't another German for miles. They also assure me that this hotel mostly takes locals and that they were very surprised to find me here.

They're backpacking and getting ready to move on. Their bus leaves at 7, so I don't want to keep them long.

Even so, at this late hour I get very thorough information, tips and answers to my questions. On the spot we replan my route: I'll now ride from Bishkek via Naryn to Osh. At Sary-Tash I turn left and follow the road to the green border with China.

That way I get to see more of the country, and I save hard cash too, because crossing the pass is apparently only possible by car and with incredible amounts of red tape. By bike you don't stand a chance. And frankly, I have no desire to join in the paperwork circus anyway.

Lots of tourist agencies offer a transfer service from Bishkek or Naryn to Kashi. He quotes prices of 400 USD, and you have to (!) state the exact day of the crossing. You can't just "hop on" a ride, because the driver and all passengers are registered. And so on.

So the Torugart Pass route is _no longer_ an option for me.

Yesterday's low has completely blown away. I'm really happy that this "detour" lets me see even more of the country.
