## Tuesday, 25 August 2009

## Song-Köl - The Tragedy, Part One

![](/images/2009/08/25/3883313081.jpg)

Civilisation is rapidly bidding farewell. After Kemin there are no more melons at the roadside. Sary-Bulak is little more than a small settlement of old lorry trailers that people live in and sell a few bits and pieces from.

I've still got enough to eat and drink, so I quickly escape the curious questions and get ready for my next climb.

After I turn right onto the road to Song-Köl, the road gets unbelievably bad. I'm hoping it's just a short "weak spot" and push harder on the pedals. The tyres spin, my front wheel lifts off and I have to call on all my technical skills to make it to the top in one piece.

The view makes up for the hardship, but also shows me what lies ahead. That was just the warm-up. The road winds through a dry valley. The real mountains are still far, far away.

Barely have I had a chance to let the breathtaking panorama sink in when two minibuses pull up behind me. Moments later, Spanish chatter and the stench of cheap cigarettes.

Without further ado I get back on the bike and follow the road to the next village. Despite the heat, the atmosphere there is strangely cool. People don't say hello, whisper in my direction and glare after me. I'm glad when I'm out again. And so my last hope of stocking up before the mountains is dashed too.

As I'm mulling this over and climbing the next hill, a little boy appears out of nowhere at the left side of the road on the rise. He's holding up a blade of grass. I take it as a joke and duck under the imaginary finishing line, when he yells at me: STOI! (= STOP!).

I calmly keep pedalling. Stopping is completely out of the question. With a deep "Kak stoi?! Nichevo nyet!!" (= What do you mean "stop"?! No way!) I wave him off and shortly afterwards I'm left in peace again. What was that all about?, I wonder, as nothing happens a few metres further on either.

As it gets dark, I look for a safe place to camp on a hilltop. What if that brat goes hunting tourists with his farm boys? I prepare for the worst and dream of wild chases.

Tomorrow it's up to the pass, in the hope that someone up there has something to eat. I feel extremely uneasy.
