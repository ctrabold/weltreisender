## Tuesday, 18 August 2009

## High Mountains, an Ice-Cold Silver River and Lots of Tea

![](/images/2009/08/16/3839077407.jpg)

The trip to the Ala Archa gorge was indescribably beautiful - and far too short!

My plans to stay another day in the gorge and enjoy the mountain panorama are scrapped, though, when a gang of Kyrgyz turn up in minibuses and absolutely swamp our campsite.

Relaxing is out of the question. Lesley summed it up nicely afterwards: it's a strange feeling to suddenly become the main attraction in the middle of this glorious national park.

The tent and bike are examined in minute detail. Thousands of questions are asked. One Kyrgyz man even dares to take a ride on the fully loaded Rondolf (my bike)! I turn down his request to ride for five minutes and tell him to just go round in a circle here. Looking glum, he gets on the bike.

After barely 10 seconds the show is over and he climbs off on wobbly knees. He slinks off towards the summit, looking rather small.

![](/images/2009/08/16/3839067067.jpg)

Riding with Ben and Lesley is very pleasant, the pace just right. You can really feel the thin mountain air. With plenty of breaks and an easy pace, though, we have no problems.

We stop very often to take in the scenery. Views that blow even a mountain grump like me away.

On top of that, an ice-cold river rushes through the valley, its milky-white glacier water shimmering in the sun like a silver ribbon.

Along the way there are lots of little magazins (shops) and teahouses. For lunch I buy a sugar-sweet honeydew melon at a market in Bishkek, which we then enjoy surrounded by high mountains. For supper there's delicious lagman, and as a midnight feast, Thai noodles from Christian's hobo stove.

Unfortunately, because it was Sunday, it was pretty busy, which took some of the fun out of the ride, especially on the road. The constant hooting gets on your nerves, even if it's often meant kindly.

The ride back, on the other hand, was a lot faster. It's downhill all the way with very little traffic. The road surface is better than average. In places there's even whisper-quiet tarmac!

In the evening we find a nice, spacious gostinitsa and move into our three-person apartment. Afterwards, over good food, tea and kvass, we celebrate my three-month anniversary at our regular restaurant.

I've been on the road for exactly three months! That first determined push on the pedals feels like it was yesterday.

What next?

There are a few bureaucratic hurdles to clear before I can carry on towards China. Sadly, you can't just cross the Torugart Pass as a cyclist.

Still, I'm sticking to my planned route and will travel via Naryn to Kashi / Kashgar.
