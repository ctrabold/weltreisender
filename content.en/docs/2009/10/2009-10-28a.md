## Wednesday, 28 October 2009

Made It to Xining

Today, in the best of moods, I'm heading right into the hustle and bustle of China!

I'm leaving the quiet, wide-open landscape of Qinghai for good and sweeping down at a brisk pace into the provincial capital - the first sizeable city since Volgograd!

And just as it was getting really lovely: from Delingha onwards, life returns to the daily routine of cycling. The dusty grey sandscape disappears. Everything around me gets much greener and livelier. Horses, sheep and yaks line my field of view. A real treat after the long, empty kilometres in the desert.

Still, the wasteland has left its mark. After Wulan I hit the wall: I'm completely drained, demotivated, empty. My legs don't want to pedal any more, and my head fills up with the wildest ideas and arguments for why I'm allowed to get off the bike right here and now. There must be some other way of getting to Xining - without torturing myself metre by metre through the valley against the biting wind.

Gruelling hours pass until I stop for a break in a small town near the coal mine. The warm tea in the draughty room makes me forget the icy temperatures for a moment. Then it's on uphill towards Tianjun.

Halfway there, a Buddhist place of prayer invites me to stop again. The extra helping of incense sticks and the golden statues in the rock grotto release new strength, and I surprise myself with a fabulous burst of speed on the climb.

Like a new man I polish off the next pass, 3,843 metres high, then freewheel easily for 30 kilometres down to Tianjun, overjoyed and enjoying the spectacular view into the wide, open valley.

Countless herds of yaks graze in the evening sun, frozen lakes shimmer and snow-capped mountains line the horizon. Goosebumps - this time not from the -1 degrees Celsius, but because the hardship is "rewarded" for once. For the first time in hundreds of kilometres.

I leave my inner couch potato behind on the other side of the mountain, have a hearty supper with a Chinese teacher, and the next morning set off without any trouble and ride comfortably all the way to Gangca.

The wide view over the deep blue lake, lots of smiling people at the roadside and euphorically cheering motorcyclists with huge goggles, long coats and blue balaclavas with a big bobble on top turn cycling into a celebration.

I hardly notice the 3,448-metre hill to Gangca. After it, though, there's almost 70 kilometres of snappy descent through the deep green valley to Huangyuan.

The bumpy road demands maximum concentration. Bumps and ruts force my eyes onto the road. And yet the view of the mountains is often so beautiful that I want to stop.

But from Huangyuan onwards the traffic increases drastically. There's so much hooting that I almost lose my senses. Often you can't even tell where the hooting is coming from. "Hold your line, no matter what" has served me very well so far.

In Xining I quickly find a good, cheap youth hostel - even with WiFi and a little lounge! At last I can load new music (and games) onto my iPod! :)

Tomorrow is a rest day = do the washing, see the city, gather my strength. ... and plan my onward journey. A tiresome topic that keeps scurrying round my head.

With a big glass of milk in my hand, I send lots and lots of greetings home!
