## Wednesday, 21 October 2009

## Ice-Cold Sandblasters

The last few days have been one thing above all: VERY cold!

![](/images/2009/10/20/white-brown-sand-land_4057671152_o.jpg)

![](/images/2009/10/12/long-road_4018859013_o.jpg)

The nights are starry and clear. In the mornings I wake up at -10 degrees Celsius and below. During the day the thermometer barely climbs above 10 degrees. The wind is icy. Out of the wind, the sun warms you a little.

![](/images/2009/10/05/leopard-road--leopardenstrae_4019420978_o.jpg)

In the evenings a small fire now and then (I found a few bits of wood in the sand) allows a few romantic minutes in the desert night, but as soon as the sun has gone we quickly crawl into the tent.

I feel well equipped - I knew it would get cold - and thanks to my good clothing I hardly feel the cold.

The sleeping bag is at its limit, but still warm enough. At night I put my jackets over it and stuff the foot end with my cycling shorts. I keep my jersey on. That way I'm warmly dressed in the morning in no time.

All body parts are fine and fully intact.

Still, because of these conditions I've decided to give further mountains a miss and head for the warmer south as quickly as possible.

In the desert, for days on end there's nothing to see for miles but sand and rock. It's made me have a few doubts, because I'm "using up" my China visa in this wasteland and haven't seen much of China at all yet.

I'm currently in [Delingha](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delingha), trying to extend my visa by another 30 days.

As the road is longer than I originally thought, I decide to take the night bus. Ron comes with me. Noel carries on alone. We're planning to continue together from Delingha.

And so Ron and I wait for five hours in a small settlement in the middle of the desert for the night bus to Delingha. Until shortly after 7 pm it's unclear whether the bus even stops here and whether it'll take us.

At 7:13 pm two women shoo us away from the dinner table and signal that the bus stops in front of the shop at 7:30 pm. We quickly pay for our food and beer and get our bikes ready for the journey.

And sure enough: the bus stops on time, and after a bit of discussion our bags are stowed and the bikes are hanging between the bunks. The whole thing is watched by dozens of Chinese eyes staring sleepily at us from the windows.

As a consolation, we sit right at the front next to the driver, enjoy the star-studded desert sky to the gentle hum of the engine, and are given fruit and good advice for the onward journey.

Around 11 pm the mood gets quieter when, in the middle of the dark night, a big mound of sand suddenly appears - on the road! Without any warning (!), the road is closed from here. No sign, no lights, nothing!

![](/images/2009/10/08/lichtblick_4031975572_o.jpg)

Trying to switch to the side road, the bus gets stuck sideways in the sand. With combined effort we heave it out of the hole again. The crack in my right shoe sole has got a bit bigger again. Completely covered in dust and exhausted, we drive on.

A little later we're on the side road, chatting as best we can with the other passengers about our trip. Then there's a thud and the bus runs over a young deer. Without further ado it's heaved into the bus and off we go.

Because of all this we only reach Delingha at around 2 am, in pitch-black night. A ghost town awaits us. It's teeming with flashing hotels, but none of them will take us - even the "tourist hotels" refuse.

Very annoying, and hard to swallow despite what other China travellers had told us: even in the hotels "approved" for tourists, so far NOBODY speaks even a word of English. Luckily a small dictionary helps us out.

In five hotels, sleepy receptionists wave us away half-heartedly… until Ron's patience snaps. Thanks to his somewhat brusquer manner we end up with a luxury suite after all and can finally shower, warm up and relax.

In the morning it's straight to the police station. Luckily the officer speaks a bit of English. Even so, everything takes forever. The printer's playing up... we're told to come back tomorrow.

The officer also makes it clear to us that we're in an area closed to tourists. Looking it up on the English Wikipedia page explains: "It is closed off to foreigners due to nuclear testing." (<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delingha>)

Once I've got the new visa in my pocket, I'll travel by bike, bus or train via Xining towards Lanzhou (<http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzhou>).
