## Tuesday, 8 December 2009

Thoughts on a Train Journey

Tomorrow morning just before 6 we arrive in Kunming. Kunming - a big, important milestone on my trip! There I'll get the remaining visas for the countries ahead and set off for South-East Asia.

Otherwise I have everything I need and feel well equipped. I've only missed a few things / bits and pieces so far.

Things I've bought along the way:

- 1 x walking sandals
- 1 x light shoes without laces (wandering through a dirty city in sandals is grim)
- 1 x reusable chopsticks
- 1 x USB SD card reader. Saves the camera battery. Only a few computers have an SD slot.
- 2 x mini thermometers
- 1 x mini compass and LED light for my trouser pocket (good for finding your way around town)
- 1 x mini speaker for the iPod
- 1 x black sports socks. Black just looks better ;)
- Various maps (important: go for maps in the local language!)
- new music and apps for the iPod Touch

What's really VERY nice is that thanks to the iPod Touch I can download new music, games and dictionaries. That's absolutely brilliant! If the battery lasted longer it would be even better, but so far that's rarely been a real problem.

- Various dictionaries / phrasebooks

What I've really missed, but couldn't find in any bookshop in China, is "Kauderwelsch Chinesisch" (a German phrasebook series). The "Kauderwelsch Russisch" helped me no end in Russia. I often thought: "If only I'd brought this little book along"... This communication aid would have been really useful, especially at the beginning. It makes an enormous difference if you can speak a bit of Chinese - really unbelievable!

Things I've hardly or never used so far:

- International plug adapter. So far I've been able to plug in our two-pin plugs everywhere. That really surprised me.
- Walking boots (they've been back in Germany since Kashgar)
- Address stamp (you have to fill in every form by hand)
- Set square, mini games, ink
- 2nd "warm" T-shirt. The winter clothes and the tent are on their way home.
- Multi-tool, whereas the micro-tool is in almost constant use (scissors, bottle opener, nail care)
- Razor :-D

In hindsight the tent isn't really suitable for a bike tour - too big, too heavy and too fiddly to put up. I preferred Noel's tent (Big Agnes SeedhouseSL2), not only because he ALWAYS had it up faster than me :)

In general I'm feeling very strongly at the moment how little I fancy all these tourist attractions. All the places you simply MUST have been to… awful.

They don't appeal to me at all (they're usually overcrowded, exhausting and expensive). A bit of a shame, really, because with the new, long visa I'd have the chance to see a lot. On top of that, winter is coming in China, and with it cold and unfriendly weather.

I'm longing for more green and less traffic. So I'm sticking to the original plan and making sure I get down to the south.

I've been on the road for almost seven months now, and I still often feel as if it's only just really beginning. I'm thrilled about the new countries, but I'm trying to keep my expectations as low as possible (Kyrgyzstan syndrome).

Finally, a thought for anyone planning a visit to China: if I ever travel to China again, I'll go via Hong Kong. Get the 90-day China visa there and party off the jet lag. Then take your time through the New Territories (enjoy the beaches and the nature!) up to the mainland, or to Macau (I sadly skipped a visit there, but it's supposed to be interesting). Then travel west towards Yunnan, or fly from Shenzhen or Guangzhou to Beijing… or, or, or.
