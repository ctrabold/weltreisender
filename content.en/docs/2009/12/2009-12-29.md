## Tuesday, 29 December 2009

New Year's Eve in the Seven Mountains

​29 December 2009 to 3 January 2010

Pictures: [Guangxi]({{< relref "/tags/guangxi" >}})

Two more sleeps, then it's New Year's Eve!

Next to my bed, though, there's already a little firework display today: a capacitor burns out in my USB charger. Pop! When I wake up it no longer works, and I can probably forget about my iPod for the long train journey.

Hunting for a replacement in Kunming and Guilin is nerve-racking - not least because of my lack of language skills - but in the end I find a decent replacement in Guilin that actually works.

In the evening Daniel and I go out for a nice meal and enjoy it with a good Dornfelder.

The next morning we set off just after 8: looking at mountains, marvelling at the Chinese and dodging the tourist rip-off merchants.

Despite the dull weather and a slight hangover, the trip down the Li River to Yangshuo is lovely and entertaining. Daniel and I join a Chinese tour group (because it's cheaper and potentially more fun too) and enjoy the boat trip of several hours through the glorious scenery. There's always something to see.

Unfortunately, by New Year's Eve my batteries are a bit flat from all the impressions and influences, and even a little recovery nap doesn't change that much.

On top of that, there's absolutely no New Year's Eve atmosphere in town. OK - a few foreigners spontaneously let off some fireworks and bawl the countdown - which I find rather embarrassing - but otherwise it's dead as a doornail as far as New Year's spirit goes. Strange.

The short nights before have left me unexpectedly sleepy, and I give in to it with a slightly guilty conscience. Shortly after 1 am we're in bed.

Instead, on New Year's morning we cycle, fresh and lively, to Moon Hill - an AAAA top scenic spot that's genuinely impressive and, by Chinese standards, even fairly quiet.

On the second day of the New Year we whizz along the Li River on Segways and enjoy the wonderful scenery, "floating". After a short practice session, the Segway is impressively easy and intuitive to ride. Brilliant! So, if anyone
still hasn't got me a Christmas present... :)

In the evening we watch the cormorants night fishing. Ultra-touristy, but still impressive.

Then everything happens very quickly: Sunday is the day we head back, and we take a taxi to the airport in Guilin. There I say goodbye to Daniel and travel on to Guilin station, where just before 5 pm I take my seat on the train back to Kunming.

The return journey is much calmer than the trip out. With R.E.M.'s "Drive" in my ears, I lie on my back on my bunk, gaze at the starry sky and watch the shadows of the karst hills rush past.

A lovely farewell to China!
