## Sunday, 26 July 2009

## Another lesson in Hotelese and Lorry-speak

![](/images/2009/07/30/3820478780.jpg)

Just after 9 the cleaning lady is standing in my room and I fall out of bed in fright.

Hair flying, I hiss her out of the room. She's brutally torn me out of my sleep.

Note to self: always hang the red sign on the door straight away!

Last night I spent ages at the computer in the "business centre", trying to upload the new pictures and write to you.

Suddenly the connection is cut and my texts can't be saved any more. Uploading more pictures isn't possible any more either (really annoying without the Flash plugin, by the way!). Every picture has to be uploaded individually. No fun with over 100 pictures.

As I find out after checking with reception, my account is only valid for one hour. I throw in the towel for today and go to bed frustrated. To round the day off, I enjoy a drinking yoghurt while looking out over the harbour at night, and it sits in my stomach like a stone all night long. Once again I sleep very badly.

And then this rude wake-up call! In the lift I then see that breakfast is only served until 10 - not until 11, as I'd thought.

So I get on the bike without breakfast and say goodbye to the Danes.

The first few metres lead through a quiet city park, past the restaurant where we enjoyed excellent shashlik last night. Then it's on through heavy city traffic towards the mountains.

The sun is scorching, and today too I've got my 4-litre water bladder on the rack. That proved its worth in the Crimea. The hard shoulder is as bumpy as usual, but I'm making good progress.

When suddenly a lorry without an exhaust drives right past me, I think my last hour has come. The pistons are hammering straight into my ear canal and it's brutally loud. The black cloud of exhaust blows into my face, unfiltered.

Note to self: as a cyclist you have to be prepared for anything!

After the shock and a few more metres of climbing, the road calms down and the gradients get gentler. It's going brilliantly!

On the other side of the road I see a policeman checking a lorry driver. Behind the lorry, a queue of more lorries, buses and cars. After 8 kilometres I stop measuring. _Whyyy, though?_

At just after 4 pm I reach Abinsk and find a nice room with a Russian family. The babushka is lovely and I can make her laugh no end with my Russian. As a thank you she gives me a few blackberries from her wonderful garden.

Shortly afterwards I fall into a comatose sleep and completely sleep through the lovely weather outside. Only during the night am I tormented so badly by one (!) mosquito that I don't get back to sleep until half past four.

I look ahead anxiously to the rest of the way to Krasnodar. How am I supposed to build up enough strength for tomorrow's ride like this?
