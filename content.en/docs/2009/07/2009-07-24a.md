## Friday, 24 July 2009

## Happy reunion

![](/images/2009/07/24/3769581500.jpg)

After a quick snack at the Wi-Fi café I decide to head for the harbour at Port Krym. From there a ferry crosses to Russia every two hours.

The road there goes on forever. Lots of Russians race past me. It's just before half past seven. Apparently there's still one more ferry. Will I make it?

With my last ounce of strength I pedal against the last Crimean hills and, red in the face, I still reach Port Krym while it's light.

Even so, the whole thing seems utterly unplanned. Because even if I caught the next ferry, I'd arrive on the other side at night, somewhere in Russia. My map shows no towns, hotels or campsites within reach.

![](/images/2009/07/24/3769581536.jpg)

Even from a distance I can see the long queue in front of a little hut, and I join it expectantly. People gawp at me, whisper and walk round me to cut their waiting short. Meanwhile I try to drink from my water bag as confidently as possible, because the pizza has made me very thirsty.

I've no idea how things work here and just hope it'll all be fine. Above the hut it says "Kassa" - a glimmer of hope. There are also lots of border officials walking around, and inside it looks like passport control.

Unfortunately the entrance door is so narrow that I doubt I'll simply get through with the bike. Once again I picture the huge palaver of squeezing Rondolf through doorways. The world just isn't designed for cycle tourists.

As I'm staring at the entrance, working out the best angle of approach for the bike, a familiar figure strolls out of the building.

It's the Danish driver of the red VW! We greet each other overjoyed. He'd also wanted to check out the port in advance and has already gathered a few tips. First you have to buy a ticket at the Kassa. Only then is it worth joining the queue for passport control. Suddenly everything is clear and simple.

After a word with his wife, we load Rondolf into the car once again and go looking for somewhere to stay the night together.

Moments like these are very hard to describe. The crazy decision to ride to the harbour, the beads of sweat on the last hills, skipping another break before the port... all of a sudden everything makes sense again and feels right and easy.

After a long search in muggy Kerch we find a hotel. Over dinner we decide to cross the border as a threesome.

A huge weight falls from my shoulders and I go to bed full of confidence. The three of us will definitely manage the border crossing!
