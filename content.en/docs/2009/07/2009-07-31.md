## Friday, 31 July 2009

## A dreamy place to sleep on the Don

![](/images/2009/07/30/3820496082.jpg)

I sleep more peacefully and relaxed than I have in ages! Fresh and rested, I wake up on the banks of the Don. All around me silence, glorious blue and lush grass.

Saying goodbye to the Danes yesterday was short and heartfelt. There was sparkling wine at the breakfast buffet, and we toasted our travels properly and feasted well.

After that I headed out of the city along lonely roads. First on a busy road, then on farm tracks, over hill and dale along the banks of the Don. The track is so smooth that it's very easy to ride. The peace and seclusion are a real tonic for my lorry-battered ears.

For hours there isn't a car in sight. Now and then I ride through a little village, where I cause a stir and experience some very funny situations. Horn concerts, waving children and heartily laughing babushkas at the roadside.

At 5 pm it's beddy-byes time, and right on cue I find a lovely spot on the riverbank and, without much thought, pitch my tent not far from Arpachin.

![](/images/2009/07/30/3819692823.jpg)

![](/images/2009/07/31/3819726621.jpg)

The rest of the way to Semikarakorsk then drags on like chewing gum again. On top of that, the quiet farm track ends abruptly and joins a main road that leads over the only bridge for miles around.

As if by a miracle, I catch a quiet spell and make it safely to the other side. After I've stopped to let my pulse settle, lorries and Ladas thunder past me again.

It's really strange: the traffic is very irregular. It comes in waves, rather. So even on main roads there are minutes of absolute calm, which are then flattened all the harder by the next wave of traffic.

This irregularity is what makes the riding so exhausting. You've barely relaxed when the next stressful phase arrives. Add to that the steadily worsening state of the roads.

Towards evening, after hours of asking around, I find a gostinitsa (guesthouse). The babushka seems friendly, but a bit disturbed. I quickly move into my double room, have a shower and put my headphones on. I've had enough traffic noise in my ears for one day.

Just as I'm about to doze off, the door opens and a big, stocky man is standing in the room.

The babushka points at the bed. The man agrees, puts his things down and disappears again. I'm confused, but introduce myself politely. We soon get talking and swap thoughts about travelling. He's from the south of Russia (near Georgia) and urges me to make a detour there.

I fall asleep with new travel plans.
