## Wednesday, 29 July 2009

Forced cabin fever

My mood is lousy. At breakfast I start feeling queasy. My throat is scratchy. My heart is pounding hard against my chest. I feel awful. Everything's c*ap.

So I take another day off and spend the whole day in my room.

My original plans to explore the city are thwarted alternately by my mood and then by the weather:

In the morning I still fancy resting a bit and letting the sun shine on my face. When I finally drag myself out for a walk, there's thunder and lightning outside. In between, heavy rain again and again, which even soaks my things that I'd hung out to dry on the balcony yesterday.

I try to take days like this in my stride, and I think, eat eat eat and sleep a lot. The audiobooks and games on the iPod help to kill time. I don't set foot outside the door today!

This intensive "standstill" treatment rekindles my desire to cycle - or so I hope. And yes, I do want to cycle, to explore the area, but I can also feel that I've got a moderately strong mental block.

So this morning I gratefully accept the Danes' offer and ride along with them once more towards Rostov.

During the drive I let out several deep sighs: the road is overcrowded, very narrow, and the weather is horrible.

It would have been a very dangerous ride out of Krasnodar, and I'm glad to be able to zoom north in the Red Rocket and talk and laugh with the two of them.

The landscape is steppe = veeery wide and unspectacular. From the passenger seat I get an impression of the scenery awaiting me over the next few days, as I'm planning to ride from Rostov along the back roads a little further south-east again, along the Don, and then north towards Volgograd.

We stop at a motorway service station. Rasmus orders, and the whole place watches and listens to us as he does. We're curious to see what we'll get.

The mood: any moment now the door will slam shut and vampires will pounce on us. But nothing of the sort happens, and shortly afterwards the friendly waitress brings us the dishes we ordered.

In fits of laughter we eat our shashlik, then leave the dive with tears in our eyes and in the best of moods.

Just before the city limits our papers are checked. A bureaucratic act that happens again and again and is completely normal. The police are omnipresent and check thoroughly. Long queues are nothing unusual. Even so, the atmosphere is always calm and friendly. It's just normal.

In the city alone, while looking for our hotel, we're checked three more (!) times within half an hour... but in return we get valuable tips on how to get to the hotel.

Once again the hotel blows my travel budget and is extremely posh. From tomorrow there'll be a very different wind blowing through my travel kitty, I swear, as my head falls onto the flannel-soft pillow.
