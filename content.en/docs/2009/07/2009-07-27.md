## Monday, 27 July 2009

## A rollercoaster of emotions deluxe, ready for print

![](/images/2009/07/27/3819666939.jpg)

Weak and not at all well fed, I set off towards Krasnodar.

The sky is overcast, the change in the weather is weighing on my soul. I feel lousy.

The road has completely changed too. Yesterday it carried me gently and smoothly through the countryside; today it snaps at me with gaping potholes and makes progress hard with missing hard shoulders.

Broken glass and dirt everywhere. After half an hour the first drops fall too, and the potholes fill up with water.

The mix of exhaust fumes, horns, grey sky, dirt and free showers from the left is simply awful. How often I wish the cosy VW van would turn up! A few times I stop and look back, hoping to see it. These are some of the most brutal metres of my trip.

After two hours my morale is broken and I leave the main road for the supposedly quieter country road towards Severskaya, where I stock up on sausage, cheese and bread in a little shop.

As I step out of the shop, the heavens open. Before I've even got my rain gear on, I and my shopping are soaked.

Luckily I had plenty of practice at this in the Czech Republic, so I calmly fix my gaze on the middle distance and chew my bread contentedly. I think of the scent of the Crimea and the sun.

The beautiful images bring back my enthusiasm, and a little later I roll into the centre. Getting any further, though, is more a case of aimless wandering.

As it's still raining, I decide to spend the time in an internet café. And what luck: shortly afterwards I find a big sign saying "Internet Club". But what a shock: the network isn't working.

A little later I find myself in the entrance of a building surrounded by nothing but women. Looking for the interwebs, I'd followed a sign whose design reminded me of such places.

The women are beside themselves and utterly delighted. They ask all sorts of questions and write down my answers. I'm getting suspicious, but I trot out my best gibberish Russian phrases: I'm looking for an internet café, or at least a map of the area.

As it turns out, I've stumbled straight into the editorial office of the local newspaper. In no time the group photo has been taken. Even though an hour later I still have neither internet nor a map: it's balm for my battered morale!

When the editor-in-chief then shows me the way to the motorway in her Lada, my desire to ride on gets back to almost normal levels.

She leads me onto the very road I'd taken right at the start - and where I'd turned back because it seemed wrong...

Looking back, I'm very grateful for this "detour" and really curious how they'll embellish the story of the stranger from afar.

Fortified, I tackle the last 25 kilometres, but I can tell there won't be any great leaps today. I'm mentally and physically empty.

As I decide to soothe my mood with sweets at the next petrol station, a chorus of horns sounds behind me.

The Danes drive past me laughing and waving. Tears of joy shoot into my eyes and I'm so overwhelmed that I even have to hug Rasmus by way of greeting (a bit rude in hindsight, as my clothes are wet and disgusting).

I stammer something or other and without further ado we load the bike in again. Warmth! Thank you!

Today too our teamwork pays off. We find our way around the hectic city well and check into good rooms at the Intourist Hotel Krasnodar.

Tomorrow, though, our paths will part for good. They're going to try to take the train to Volgograd, as they're running out of time. I'll continue my bike tour on the back roads, hoping the riding will be calmer there.

Chats with a few Russian drivers make me optimistic: the route I've chosen to Volgograd is said to be very quiet and beautiful.
