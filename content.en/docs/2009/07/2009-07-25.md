## Saturday, 25 July 2009

## Russia is here here here

![](/images/2009/07/25/3819639117.jpg)

The day passes in a blur: just after five I wake up, repack my things, get out the important documents for passport control and check once more that everything's there. I'm very nervous.

After a meagre breakfast we stock up on travel provisions at the nearby supermarket.

Then we drive to the harbour. In no time we've got our tickets, and an hour later we're already at the first passport control.

Everything goes smoothly there too, and just under two hours later we're on the boat. The crossing only takes 20 minutes and leaves no time to relax. The refreshing sea breeze has barely dried our sweat when we're already on the Russian side. There it's straight on with the customs declaration, passport control, security check and car insurance.

The sweat is dripping again - the thermometer reads 37 degrees Celsius.

The customs declaration is entirely in Russian and gives us a few headaches. Luckily a Russian woman who speaks German comes to the rescue and translates the terms for us. So we clear this hurdle too.

At passport control we step up to the counter together as "Team Red Rocket" and get our stamps without any problems.

Once the car insurance is sorted too, we leave the border waving and head straight for the nearest beach: breakfast and a swim.

The sea is as warm as a bath and stays very shallow for a long way out. I sit in the warm water for a few silent minutes, looking west towards the Crimea, towards the road behind me. The impressions are enormous. Maybe at some point I'll be able to "process" them properly. Right now I'm far too fidgety, and somehow everything is happening so fast that I don't really get round to reflecting:

For the first time I'm standing on Russian soil, swimming in Russian water... I'm across the border! A huge weight falls from my shoulders. The others feel much the same.

We were a great team and at times it was even really good fun.

The car is a real attraction and the border officials have a soft spot for it. We certainly had to answer at least as many questions about the car as about ourselves.

As we set up the table on the beach, a couple pulls up next to us and wants to photograph the car. Very excited, they film us having breakfast with the van and are over the moon.

Then, fortified, we drive over the Caucasus towards Novorossiysk. The landscape is, and stays, impressive, and the state of the roads even improves a little.

The mountains do fill me with a healthy respect, though. Suddenly I feel very weak. Driving in the car has given me a deceptive sense of distance, which from tomorrow, like it or not, I'll have to scale back again.

Tomorrow our paths part. The Danes want to spend their rest day on their own. And I want to sniff the tarmac air again too.
