## Thursday, 2 July 2009

Polish butter biscuits and fat dogs

Today is a rest day.

A little way out of town I've found a lovely campsite and pitched my tent with a view over the valley. Above my tent, fat beetles are buzzing in the fruit tree.

Getting here yesterday was - despite a modest 15 kilometres - a little adventure in itself:

On the map, four kilometres as the crow flies at most. If it weren't for that one hill in between. Combined with the misleading town map, on which the route is drawn so clearly and plainly straight ahead, the result is yet another absurd ride, or rather "search for the way".

When the path suddenly turns into a narrow forest track with a 45% gradient, my ambition kicks in and I decide to try it anyway. When Rondolf and I then slide back down the slippery ground despite the brakes being on, there's literally no stopping me, and I give it everything to conquer the (ridiculous) hill.

Shortly afterwards the footpath crosses a tarmac road and I climb out of the head-high grass back into the civilised world.

Satisfied and soaking wet, I reach the summit of the 285-metre-high hill. You've got to be able to enjoy the little things in life ;)

It's completely quiet up here. Only a gentle breeze brushing lightly over the grassland. And there's a lovely, intense smell of summer meadow.

On the next bench I fall into a deep afternoon nap and wake up almost an hour later, drenched in sweat. The sun is scorching.

I follow the path to some old ruins, climb the viewpoint there and enjoy the view across the wide countryside around Kraków.

Half an hour later I'm at the campsite, where I'm warmly welcomed. The young woman at reception studied Russian. My tent neighbour has cycled here from Helsinki, and the family opposite is from Lithuania. In the distance I can hear the crooning of Hansi Hinterseer (an Austrian schlager singer). So the Germans aren't far away either.

I spend today on the sun terrace with Żywiec (beer), Polish butter biscuits and the iPod.

As I write, the campsite's little fat dogs keep scurrying around me, begging for a biscuit.

In a moment I'll have to get the battery charged at reception again. Time to carry on learning Russian. ;)
