## Saturday, 18 July 2009

## What's kvass, then?

![](/images/2009/07/18/3756347658.jpg)

The train to Simferopol leaves at 23:29. It's just after 10 am now. The sun is shining. I've had a good breakfast and I'm lying in bed for a bit longer. All's well, really.

Even though I don't have to check out until 12, I still feel strangely rushed. The clatter of the cleaning crew is getting closer and closer.

Around midday I take a break in a park and have a go at two crosswords. A little later a genuine babushka (granny) sits down right next to me.

I flinch slightly, keep staring at the puzzle and secretly hope she'll move on soon. I'm not in the mood for superficial chit-chat, which so far has all too often ended in begging.

Her name is Luba, she's 70 years old and a thoroughly warm-hearted person!

She speaks excellent English and we talk a lot about her life, Ukraine and my trip.

I listen intently to her stories about Tashkent in Uzbekistan, where she lived for a long time. "Odessa is muuuch nicer!" she laughs.

When my fingers wander across Kazakhstan on the map, she urgently advises me to fly over that stretch. At the time of year I'm aiming for, travelling overland through the sandstorms and the heat would be life-threatening.

I believe her, and when she sets off to take food to her homeless dogs, I feel a little pang. Thank you for the lovely chat, Luba! I'll take your advice.

A little later I make my way to the station. The thermometer reads 38 degrees Celsius.

The train pulls into the station a good hour before departure. So there's plenty of time to load up calmly and find my compartment. Thanks to Yuri, I've optimised my strategy - or rather my smile - for female train attendants, so this time Rondolf travels for free. :)

To my horror I'm put in a compartment with four berths! My hope of a nice one-to-one chat in a comfortable two-berth compartment as a birthday present fades.

The train is also noticeably older and more worn than on my first trip. I've barely sat down when three Ukrainians surround me and unleash a storm of words above my head. One of them, with his rugged face, had already caught my eye while we were waiting. I'm drenched in sweat.

The situation is actually quite simple, but thanks to the language barrier it keeps us busy almost all evening:

1. The father wants to sleep in the compartment with his son.
2. The two friends want to be with the father so they can chat.

I'm one "too many". After a lot of back and forth, the five of us share the compartment until bedtime. Then one of them disappears into his bunk.

As the clock strikes twelve, I'm sitting between three half-naked Ukrainians who are tucking into a hearty supper, drinking vodka and bombarding me with questions. I clink glasses with them with my beer. I turn down the invitation to eat and drink with them.

![](/images/2009/07/18/3756395810.jpg)

Until late into the night I try to answer their questions and listen to their language. Most of all, though, we laugh a lot.

I'm slowly starting to think that trains are a wonderful way to get to know Ukraine.

Just before six they get off, and it gets loud again.

We reach Simferopol just before two in the afternoon, in glorious weather. It's hot. Loud marching music is blaring from the loudspeakers. With practised ease I unload and note proudly: that's the second train journey survived, too.

![](/images/2009/07/19/3769175024.jpg)

Despite a long sleep I feel a bit short of sleep. Even so, I ride straight on along the fairly busy N 06 towards Sevastopol.

![](/images/2009/07/19/3769197920.jpg)

In Bakhchysarai I stop for a break at a little bar with a pretty outdoor area. On offer: квас (kvass).

This time I dare to take my first sip of the mysterious drink, which is sold from big yellow tanks on almost every street corner.

For fear of stomach trouble or the like from the local brew, I'd banned myself from trying this speciality. _Nothing ventured, nothing gained_: the taste is sensational and beats Kofola by a mile!

Armed with a big bottle of квас, three glasses later I find a cheap hotel, where straight after a shower I catch up on my lack of sleep.

PS: By the way, so far I haven't noticed any problems in the digestive department - quite the opposite! The fermented rye bread drink gives you energy and even tastes quite good when it's warm!
