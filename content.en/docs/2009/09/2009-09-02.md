## Wednesday, 2 September 2009

## Exploring Kyrgyzstan

![](/images/2009/09/02/3884415868.jpg)

Arrived in Jalal-Abad safe and sound. It's almost right on the Uzbek border near Osh and the first proper town in almost two weeks.

The contrast between the last few days and this lively, noisy town is pretty extreme. Now I realise even more how lonely, quiet and beautiful it was!

The days were overflowing with intense impressions, emotions and challenges. I hope to find a better internet café in Osh and describe the days in more detail then. So for now, just the key facts:

I've cycled right across Kyrgyzstan, climbed three passes over 3,000 metres and one at 2,800 metres on disastrous "roads", and outdone myself several times along the way.

It was extremely brutal; nature was merciless in terms of wind, heat and terrain.

Kyrgyzstan's scenery is very varied. From gentle green hills to desert, it has everything. The changes in landscape - especially after the passes - are stunning and highly emotional.

![](/images/2009/09/02/3883626407.jpg)

Almost every time, I pitched my tent by wild, crystal-clear rivers. That way I could wash myself and my clothes and regularly top up my water.

Only once did I have company at night, from three trekkers from Slovakia. Otherwise - apart from three overly nosy Kyrgyz kids - I was always alone, and during the day I always cycled through the landscape solo.

The most common road users are cows, sheep and donkeys. There aren't many cars on these roads - but enough to really get on your nerves with their jostling and hooting, or to put a smile on your face with funny gestures.

Contact with the locals was often more than tedious and at times extremely draining. Luckily, reactions to strangers vary a lot from region to region, so the positive impressions do (thankfully) win out - a few days ago (especially around Song-Köl lake) things looked very different.

Tonight I'm staying in a Catholic mission and tomorrow I'll try to carry on towards Osh. We'll see whether I spend another day here to buy a few treats and recharge my batteries, or whether I'll do that in Osh.

![](/images/2009/09/04/3908621129.jpg)

In Osh I'll very definitely take a longer break. Until I fancy mountains again ;) Because the pass to _Kitai_ (China) is signposted at over 4,000 metres.

Warmest wishes!
