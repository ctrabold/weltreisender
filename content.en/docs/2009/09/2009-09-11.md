## Friday, 11 September 2009

## Oh my GOsh

I've more or less acclimatised to Osh and have even grown to like the city. It's noisy and dirty, but there are lots of restaurants, shops and cash machines. The accommodation is super central and pleasant.

My feelings have calmed down a little again. So far Osh has given me far more positive experiences than the countryside - although lots of travellers share my overall impression so far and rave about the hospitality of the other "-stan" countries.

My days here are filled with eating, sleeping and shopping. This morning I extended my Kyrgyz visa by a month, so now I can take a few more days to recover without any time pressure.

I'm still not 100% fit, and ahead of me lies a series of passes that top everything so far. The border crossing into China is supposed to be at over 4,000 metres.

The weather is unusually cold for the time of year. I'm fully expecting snow and cold. On top of that, the road - especially over the last mountain - gets very bad again.

So I'll only move on once I'm fully there mentally again. The trip is extremely tiring and hard at the moment. This must be how Frodo felt in front of Sauron's fortress: small, insignificant. Surrounded by great mountains, but hoping to somehow make it and find peace and rest after all the hardship.

![](/images/2009/09/17/4030975781.jpg)

A ray of hope are the stories about Kashi / Kashgar in China, which is supposed to be well set up for tourists. There's even massage and lots and lots of good food... The roads are also supposed to get better on the Chinese side.

There really does seem to be everything a traveller's heart could desire there, **except free communication**, because the Chinese province is cut off. That means:

- no internet, only certain Chinese websites can be accessed
- no phone calls or texts abroad

So I'm expecting a long radio silence. In the next province, things should be a bit freer again...

I'm planning to get east as quickly as possible. Either by train, night bus or simply by bike. Then I'd travel via Myanmar to Thailand.

As always, that could change any day, but I can picture this route well at the moment - especially if I get the visa for Myanmar and factor in the holiday bonus of Thailand. I've crossed Tibet, India and Nepal off the list.

Keep your fingers crossed that it all works out, and don't worry if things get a bit quieter here over the next few days ;)

Salam aleikum!
