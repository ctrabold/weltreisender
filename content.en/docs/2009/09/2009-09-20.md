## Sunday, 20 September 2009

## Off to China

16 to 20 September

![](/images/2009/09/19/4018315377.jpg)

Right on time, Noel and I leave Osh heading for Gulcha. The road is in good shape up to the pass and we make good progress.

![](/images/2009/09/19/4031023337.jpg)

![](/images/2009/09/19/4018304817.jpg)

What holds us up most are the herds of livestock being driven down from the mountains into the valley. A fun bit of variety in a fairly bleak landscape.

I'm nervous, and after the long break I can feel my pulse in my throat on every climb.

In the valley it's warm and muggy; on the pass it's very fresh. Dark clouds surround us.

Our speedy descent is interrupted by roadworks. From one metre to the next the good road surface disappears and gives way to a stony, dusty track.

During a short break we decide to pitch our tents as quickly as possible. Black storm clouds hang above us. The noisy roadworks swallowed the rumbling thunder and we don't have much time left. Luckily there's a nice little spot in sight a few metres further on, and we push our bikes off the road.

As I go to push the first peg into the ground, a gale suddenly sweeps through the valley, so strong that I have to hold my tent down with both hands. Noel is wrapped up in his flysheet. Rain lashes down on us. Then pea-sized hailstones pelt us.

After 10 minutes we give up, quite literally strike camp and take shelter in the shepherd's house a hundred metres away, where we dry our soaked clothes by the warm stove over tea and bread. As thanks, we offer our bars, nuts and dried fruit.

Sadly, communication is difficult. The eldest daughter speaks a bit of English. Still, we laugh a lot and that warms our hearts too.

It keeps raining long after dark. When it stops, we say goodbye and go back to the campsite.

Noel's things are completely soaked. The inside of my tent has stayed dry, so we decide to spend the night together in my tent. Good thing I brought a two-man tent :)

In the morning everything goes much better. Despite the damp night I'm rested and raring to go. Today's goal is Sopu-Korgon, 60 kilometres away.

I'm a bit worried that I won't get my clothes dry and that everything will freeze when it gets colder outside. Luckily the sun beats the rain clouds every now and then, so the most important bits can dry out a little.

Along the way there are several tea stops and heavenly jam with fresh bread. So for a little while we can forget our wet things, shake off the road dust and recharge our batteries.

What really gets on my nerves today are the kids. In Sopu-Korgon I feel like I'm in the monkey house. Get out of here fast is the motto.

The road gets noticeably worse and we squeeze along the narrow tracks with heavy lorry traffic.

In the evening sun we find a clump of bushes not far from the road where we can hide. Unfortunately the rain and passing Kyrgyz scupper our "campfire" plan. So after supper it's early into our warm sleeping bags.

The morning of 18 September calls for a celebration. Today I've been on the road for four months and I'm on the Pamir Highway! Today we're heading up to the pass! 3,615 metres high over the Taldyk Pass towards Sary-Tash (<http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sary-Tash> / <http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alay_Valley>).

The morning sun bathes the mountains around us in warm light, and after a few warm-up exercises Noel and I are happily pedalling away. It's uphill straight away, and the biting cold soon stops being an issue.

For the first few kilometres I ride along in amazement through fascinating rock formations. Again and again the great peaks of the Pamir mountains peep out from the background. Awe-inspiring. Breathtaking. Beautiful!

At a truck stop we wolf down a big plate of lagman. Afterwards we use the strong sun to dry our things. The wind has picked up to gale force again. Before our break it was calm; now it's blowing a treat again and our things are dry in no time.

After the last village it's up to the pass. Lorries and wind whip up so much dust that visibility drops to a few metres.

During a break we meet Markus from Leipzig, Germany. He's on his way from Japan to Germany with his girlfriend.

After the break the road turns into a swamp of dust and the gradient gets noticeably steeper. The wheels sink into the finest brown dust right up to the valves. When I move over for heavier traffic, the bike gets stuck several times in dust centimetres deep. On some bends the front wheel lifts off. Breathing gets difficult. Every metre hurts.

At the pass Noel and I celebrate our arrival like a party. Our cheers can surely be heard far beyond Kyrgyzstan, and we're overjoyed to have conquered the highest pass of our trip despite the conditions. Even though dusk is falling, we take as many photos as we can.

Then the descent begins. According to the map, it's all downhill from here for the last stretch to Sary-Tash. Sadly the "stone's throw" turns into a little night ride into the unknown, because there isn't a light to be seen for miles. To top it all, there's another stiff climb.

At the end of our strength we stop at a mud-brick building and ask how far it is to Sary-Tash. Our bright appearance (bike lights and head torches) scares the Kyrgyz off at first, but then, to our great relief, we find out that we've arrived.

A few minutes later we find shelter in the warm parlour of the Gostinitsa Aida and enjoy a festive supper. We warm our frozen limbs by the stove and fall into a long sleep.

The morning of 19 September is full of the spirit of departure. Tomorrow is the end of the fasting month of Ramadan, and everywhere billy goats are being slaughtered and homes cleaned.

As we pack our bikes, our host's goat is twitching a few metres away. By the time we leave, it's already been skinned and gutted. The dog is enjoying the rest.

The night was restful and we decide to make use of the weekend to reach the Chinese border with little traffic (the border is closed at weekends).

The view from the village towards the mountains is stunning. Snow-white mountains stand before us. After a long search for the village shop, we turn onto the road towards Nura.

After a few kilometres the road becomes first-class and we glide silently past the white giants with a light tailwind. Pure cycling bliss. Almost too much to take in emotionally.

Later the road gets extremely bad again and very exhausting. My headache gets worse. When I pitch the tent, the poles don't fit properly and the wind toys with me.

Thanks to the good Ukrainian chocolate wafers from Osh, my mood improves considerably again over supper. Amazing how close the highs and lows of travelling are.

I take the mountain panorama with me into sleep and wake up the next morning full of anticipation. Is it really true that I've made it this far? Are the mountains really there?

At breakfast, gazing at this backdrop, I fall into a deep meditation and am only brought back to the present minutes later by Noel's whoops of joy.

A shepherd has ridden over with his son and lets us ride! And so I enjoy my first horse ride in front of a dream panorama.

After the gentle ride on the faithful horse come rock-hard kilometres on the Silk Road. The track is studded with sharp stones and extremely demanding.

After 42 kilometres we reach the Kyrgyz border post and carry on towards Irkeshtam (<http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irkeschtam>). Tomorrow we can cross the border. Today we enjoy one last beautiful Kyrgyz campsite, a few kilometres before the border by the red river.
