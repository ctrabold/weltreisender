## Friday, 19 June 2009

Mourning for a Stranger

Half asleep, I close the window. It's stuck. Outside it's pouring and the traffic hisses over the wet road. It's 4:33am and my head is full of half-finished dreams.

Still a mess, I shuffle down to breakfast at half past eight and shortly afterwards use a break in the rain to pack the bike quickly and start the ride with dry feet.

After 12 kilometres, though, the rain catches up with me. Then a flash of lightning. I count one, tw... and there's already a loud bang in front of me, and as if on cue extra-fat drops pelt down from the sky. In no time I'm soaking wet. Damp to the bone.

Thanks to my optimistic outlook on life (!) I'd decided against the waterproof overshoes for now, which I'm now trying, cursing, to pull over my wet shoes. With wet, clammy hands on a muddy farm track, that's no fun. Especially not when filthy lorries are also whizzing past (within arm's reach).

The day is pretty much over after barely twenty kilometres. The signposting is lousy, the rain merciless and the terrain sucks my legs dry within a few kilometres.

I use the time to come up with new curses and swear words and keep my spirits up with "at the next pub I'm drinking them dry". Unfortunately the next chance of a drink is over 15 kilometres away.

When I get there I listlessly park my bike and sit down in the parlour in my dripping clothes. I stick to two Kofolas, because somehow the mood here is odd. Uncomfortable looks come my way. I'd already noticed men in uniform outside. And in here everyone is wearing black.

A short while later funeral music starts up, and a coffin is carried through the village in a long procession. In the pub everyone is standing at the window. Only I stay seated and sip dazedly at my glass. Somehow everything "fits" today. All the bliss of the last two days seems washed away.

In the shop round the corner I top up my biscuit rations and go looking for a guesthouse. A few tough kilometres later I strike lucky, carry my bits and bobs to my room and have a hot bath. Above me the rain drums on the skylight and my clothes are drying in the next room.

The day ends after four hours and a good 62 kilometres in pouring rain in Palačov, not far from Nový Jičín. I'll have another biscuit and I'm curious to see what happens next.
