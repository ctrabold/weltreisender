## Saturday, 27 June 2009

Patience

I wake up just before 6 and pack. After a leisurely breakfast I ride to the tourist information to find out how to get out of this confusing city.

Within a few minutes it's raining again. Much to the amusement of the other tourists, I pull on my rain gear and set off in the pouring rain.

A few kilometres further on I hit the Greenways route. The joy of it makes me briefly forget the humiliating wetness I've been living in for days.

Thanks to the rest day my things had finally dried out completely again. Within a few minutes, though, that luxury is history. As a small consolation it's relatively warm and the wind is more pleasant than in the last few weeks.

The puddles are huge and treacherous. Light yellow muddy soup hides many a pothole, and Rondolf has to take a few hits.

After just a few kilometres my shoes squelch with every pedal stroke and I can feel my feet slowly swelling up.

When a dog suddenly shoots out of a driveway without warning, my patience snaps. It's at my pedal in an instant, growling. I quickly get off, land with my right foot in an ankle-deep puddle and watch the mutt run off.

Slowly and cursing, I push the bike away. A few steps later the dog's jaws, wide open, are glued to my back tyre again.

A primal scream follows and I chase the dog back into its driveway. There it stays, and "only" barks stupidly after me.

For anyone who still can't understand why in situations like this you'd love to skin these yappers on the spot: the moment of shock and the pure aggression that comes washing over you unleash primal fears and then aggression too. There's nothing of "man's best friend" left to see (cats, incidentally, handle this far more intelligently and quietly slink away).

Now my feet are well and truly wet. Downcast, I ride on.

After a few metres I feel my back wheel wobbling strangely. When I stop, I can still see the last air bubbles burbling out of the side of the tyre.

So there it is: my first puncture, in the middle of nowhere, nothing but fields and muddy puddles all around. Rain clouds above. I quickly find a spot to put my things down and start repairing the tyre.

The strong wind makes the delicate handling of tyre and repair kit impossible. Water collects in the tyre. Frustrated, I give up and push to the next road. Maybe there's somewhere to shelter there.

On the way it occurs to me that it would be much smarter to simply swap the inner tube and fix the puncture later. No sooner said than done, and 20 minutes later I'm rolling again.

In the forest, though, the tyre starts wobbling again. I curse. I'd actually missed the culprit when I felt round the tyre!

The remaining distance to Auschwitz drags on incredibly. I just can't make progress. Again and again I pump up the back tyre and hope it'll hold until I get there.

It holds, and overjoyed I check into the "Centrum" hotel. From here it's only a few metres to the Auschwitz museum, which I'll visit tomorrow.

By far the toughest day so far ends peacefully after all. Incredible.
