## Tuesday, 30 June 2009

## John Paul in the Park and Partying with a Stranger

![](/images/2009/06/30/3708837921.jpg)

After the milkshake I stroll on through town and park, somewhat revived. Even so, I'm starving and plan to have a pizza in the restaurant below my hotel and then turn in soon after.

Around the city centre there's a green belt that's perfect for a stroll.

Along the path there's a photo exhibition about John Paul II, which I walk along slowly and with interest. Many moments from his life are documented here, and some images from my younger years come back to me too.

At the last panel a young man asks me the time and abruptly pulls me out of my daydream. I'm not really in the mood for talking and deliberately keep my answers to the standard questions about where I'm from and what I think of Kraków short. But shortly afterwards I'm learning all sorts about his relationship with Germany and the world, and I sense he's desperate to talk.

After I've sketched out my route to and from Kraków, he invites me for a beer. After a moment's consideration I say yes and follow him into the rustic cellar vault of a student bar deep beneath Kraków's streets.

His name is Kristian too, and he turns out to be really entertaining company. Our sense of humour and drinking pace match, so after two beers we move on to the next bar.

Suddenly I'm in the best party mood again.

_Well deserved_, I'd say, because I've arrived safely in Kraków and so reached an important milestone unscathed. Besides, I'm sooo far east, further than ever before and... yes, I'm also extremely grateful to be able to share the thoughts and impressions of the last few weeks with someone face to face, without the click of a pay-per-minute meter.

Unfortunately the evening takes a surprisingly quick turn when suddenly I'm also supposed to pay for his cigarettes and the taxi (after all, I'd promised him earlier to pay for EVERYTHING).

Since that certainly wasn't the case and my counter-arguments don't get through to him, I end the evening by heading back to my hotel.

Rather crestfallen, I try to sort out my thoughts, which I only manage the next morning over a long breakfast.

On the one hand I could blame myself for once again not sticking to my original plan. On the other, I've seen so many interesting corners of Kraków that stay hidden from ordinary tourists. And the few extra złoty were well worth the nice hours beforehand.

The lesson I'm taking away - in addition to a thumping headache - is never again to go drinking without a decent meal inside me, and to be a bit more reserved with strangers.
