## Thursday, 11 June 2009

## Kolín, Kolín! Christiòn Is Off to Kolín

![](/images/2009/06/11/3632668852.jpg)

The day starts unusually early, just after seven, with breakfast. According to the porter it's only served from 7 to 8 (which turns out to be wrong).

The tasty rye bread with caraway makes up for it and quickly smooths out the morning creases. As always, the bread is fresh and easy on the stomach.

After a quick bike check I set off towards Nymburk. With a freshly oiled chain I carry on under my own steam. I skip the ride back to cycle route 24, since sooner or later I'll hit it again anyway. Confidently I follow route number 0019.

The four-digit number already tells me that this is another route of the fourth, "lowest" category. That means the paths are mostly unsurfaced, fairly narrow and tend to lead from village to village.

At first the path is no more than a dirt track, and apart from hoof prints I see no other tracks. Often there's barely a hand's breadth of embankment between me and the Elbe, and my adrenaline shoots up nicely once or twice.

But that's exactly what makes it so beautiful. A challenging route with no traffic, in absolute peace. River on the left, forest on the right. It goes on like this along the Elbe for several hours. How I've missed this. There are no points for the King of the Mountains here, but plenty of recovery and riding fun. Rondolf, my bike, handles beautifully and masters every obstacle with flying colours!

Later the surface changes to a perfectly built, tarmac cycle path that winds romantically through the floodplain. A paddock appears on the left and I see the horses instinctively huddling together in front of a pitch-black wall of cloud.

Because I'd been riding under the cover of the trees the whole time, I hadn't been watching the horizon. And I'd found the tailwind quite pleasant. I swallow hard and hope the rain clouds will spare me, when a downpour hits that's really something else. I sprint to the nearby farm - maybe I can shelter there until the worst is over.

Luckily the wind is driving the rain hard against the house from the west, so in the lee of the building I barely get a drop. I quickly pull on my waterproofs.

Once I'm fully wrapped up and back on the bike, it's only drizzling. "Typical!" I scoff, but keep the gear on. It's cooled down considerably, there are puddles everywhere and under the trees it's still dripping noticeably.

A few hundred metres further on I hear a loud roar. I assume it's a car behind me waiting for a good moment to overtake. I turn round and see the trees bending in the distance. The sky is even darker. And lightning is flashing out of the jet-black mass.

You can practically see the storm front raging across the land, yet I still wonder: "Will the wind come over here too?", because suddenly it's strangely still.

Of course it does. Suddenly all hell breaks loose. A hurricane-force gust grabs me and sweeps me along. From a standstill I'm doing 17 km/h. Fat raindrops lash my face and snake through every gap in my clothing. This time there's no shelter in sight. So: full steam ahead! I quickly follow the cycle path and turn off towards the Elbe.

There the full extent of the situation unfolds before me: the Elbe is raging. Normally a calm character - with a gradient of 13cm per kilometre - now waves are surging upstream, branches are flying through the air, deep thunder rumbles above me and I'm right in the middle of it.

I take my first break around 2pm, well past Nymburk. The thunder moves on.

At 4pm, after 70 kilometres and 5½ hours in the saddle, I end today's ride in Kolín, enjoy schnitzel night at the "Rabiner" and go to bed content.
