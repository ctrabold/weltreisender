## Saturday, 6 June 2009

## Travelling the Way I Prague It

![](/images/2009/06/06/3610087075.jpg)

... yes, I think Alfred Biolek, the German TV host famous for his cookery show "Kochen, wie ich es mag", would give today that title.

When I finally swung into the saddle this morning and tried to find my way out of Tábor, I see a new cycle route sign with a green logo pointing left right in front of my nose.

## Greenways Praha-Wien

I don't hesitate for long and follow the sign. At the station it even shows the distance to Prague. It's 109 kilometres. So I could arrive there on Sunday. How quickly a trip can change. Just how I like it.

After a (very meagre) breakfast I chilled out for a while on Tábor's market square and don't get out of town until around 1pm. On top of that I must have missed a turning again, because suddenly there's not a Greenways sign in sight.

After a good 10 kilometres of sightseeing tour round town I'm finally on the right path and follow the otherwise excellent signposting.

It's pitch dark all day. Thick rain clouds hang in front of the sun and over my mood. It's one of those days when you have to push hard even on the flat. But there are also descents where I almost beat my top speed of 61 km/h.

Around midday it buckets down again, and soaked to the skin I burst into a Chinese supermarket. In my makeshift English I ask for wasabi peanuts. A shrug. He only understands Czech. Very unusual. But then he points to a big jar full of the little green balls. I get a small bag filled up and ride on.

Unfortunately I then lose the _Greenways_ route and I'm thoroughly fed up. On top of that I'm frozen to the bone and simply done with the world.

The weather and the hills gnaw at my patience, and the last stretch to the hotel drags on forever. And the hotels' advertising signs turn out to be wildly inaccurate. The promised 3 kilometres are actually over 7. At least on the signs for the "Laguna" hotel, which I've picked as my resting place.

The hotel is by the lake and it's downhill all the way. "I'll have to ride all of this back up tomorrow to get back on the cycle route," I think, and still try to look forward to a hot shower.

In the restaurant there's tasty food and I watch Czech TV. The weather forecast is bad. As is the Nicolas Cage action film that follows.

When you're cycling, though, there's no switching off for now. Tomorrow I'll try to pitch my tent in Prague and then take a few rest days.
