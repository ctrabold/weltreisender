## Thursday, 18 June 2009

## Cycling, Welsh-Style

![](/images/2009/06/18/3659074017.jpg)

By 10 o'clock the bike is packed and I ride leisurely into Prostějov for breakfast.

A good twenty lads have gathered on the campsite for morning exercise and, under the teacher's loud instructions, play frisbee around my tent, which speeds up packing considerably.

The road to Olomouc is pleasantly unspectacular, and I take all the time in the world to warm up and let my thoughts wander.

Through the colourfully painted tower-block suburbs I soon reach the centre of Olomouc. There I stock up on energy bars and isotonic powder.

Over a banana milkshake in the ice-cream café on the market square, two Welshmen strike up a conversation with me. They're cycling from Venice to Kraków.

After swapping a few basics, we promptly turn our tables into a little travel centre and, delighted, spread out our maps and collected impressions for each other. They're also over the moon about the Czech Republic ("it's soooo lovely!") and are travelling the country even more by the seat of their pants than I am.

For navigation they're using a 1:1,000,000 road map. They know neither the route numbering system nor the special cycling maps. That sounds awfully familiar, and I'm glad to be able to pass on my tips. Gratefully they head off to the nearest map shop.

I ride on towards Přerov, where I find a nice guesthouse and can quickly satisfy my hunger after 69 kilometres and exactly 4:40 hours in the saddle.
