## Wednesday, 12 May 2010

Electronics on the Road

Too heavy and unnecessary: GPS, mobile phone and the chargers that go with them stay at home!

All I've got on board are my iPod Touch and the little Shuffle - and that decision really pays off!

Both pack small and can be charged via USB from a computer, without a special charger.

iPod Touch - a good, flexible travel companion (sometimes a bit too slow)

So far the iPod Touch has survived everything really well. Desert sand, heat and sweat - no problem at all!

What annoys me about the iPod Touch:

- short battery life
- it gets slow and syncing with iTunes takes forever. The music library can be unusable for up to 20 minutes.

  That happens even if you only add one new track. Very annoying.
- The constant password prompts for the iTunes Store also get on my nerves. The prompt even appears several times when you download an album, and then interrupts the download.

What's really great about the iPod Touch:

- you can add new apps and music over WiFi, depending on your mood.
- easy web surfing and emailing
- reading eBooks
- playing games or otherwise putting waiting times to (better) use

iPod Shuffle

Especially when cycling, I've come to really appreciate the little one and often wore it on my jersey sleeve.

The headphones that come with it are OK for cycling, because unlike in-ear headphones they don't muffle the ambient noise. So you still hear enough of the outside world and can move normally in traffic. Wind noise while riding is low, but sadly they offer hardly any "horn protection".

Unfortunately it takes a very long time for the battery to charge fully again.

Headphones - single point of failure

Although I packed them carefully and watertight in a Lock & Lock box, both pairs of headphones are broken.

The expensive Sennheiser headphones developed a loose connection at the plug and have been cutting out since Thailand. Otherwise they offer very good sound and excellent horn and snoring protection. Luckily another traveller gives me a spare pair of headphones.

Important with in-ear headphones: make sure you have spare ear tips! They get lost quickly and apparently aren't available separately. At any rate, I couldn't buy them separately anywhere in Southeast Asia.

Sadly I haven't been able to use the iPod Shuffle since China, because the remote control on the headphones is broken, so I can't select the volume or the tracks.

And for next time?

I'd pack exactly the same way again!

I never really missed GPS. It's sometimes interesting to know how much climbing you've done, but I don't think that justifies the extra weight. As a navigation aid a GPS device would never have paid off, because there's often a lack of good maps. A good compass is much more helpful and perfectly adequate!

A mobile phone would at most have been useful in the big cities for arranging meeting points when you have a date. In the countryside it's useless.

A charger that can be fed by solar and/or dynamo power would be very useful. One solution I'll be taking a closer look at can be found at <http://zzing.de/>.>
