## Sunday, 2 May 2010

Bye bye Malaysia! Hello Singapore!

I've arrived in Singapore safe and sound.

My journey ends here. Sadly? At last? Both.

My first impression of the city is good. Green, clean, with lots of quiet corners. Perfect for preparing for the return to Europe and getting used to things again. Just as I'd imagined and hoped.

For months now, a striking number of my thoughts have been revolving intensely around "coming home". Now "the last step" lies right in front of me. The flight home is booked, the washing done and the kit sorted out. All pretty unromantic, but necessary, and somehow nice too.

The "big final destination" arrives just like any other destination. There's no welcoming committee, no champagne shower at the town sign, no confetti. The traffic roars and flows as always. People react alarmingly normally when they see me. And the cloudburst shortly after I arrive shows me that even St Peter is busy with something else right now.

Even after more than 10,000 kilometres in the saddle, everything carries on perfectly normally - I knew that already from other cyclists' travel reports. But there are worlds between knowing it and feeling it yourself.

The joy - as so often at big moments - plays out only inside me:

A little cry of joy, a fist punched in the air and a pat on the back. Then concentrate on the road again. All a far cry from the explosive euphoria you see, for example, in football when something great happens. Goooooal!

Instead I enjoy rolling to a halt through the quiet little lanes with their pretty old houses and breathing in the atmosphere of this city. That's lovely.

As soon as I close my eyes, the images of the last months come: daydreams. Really intense. _Did I really experience all that? Was that me riding through all those countries? And how fast the time has gone!_

A sample question matrix:

Did **I** really experience all that?
Did I really experience **all that**?
Did I **really** experience all that?
Did I really experience **all** that?
Did I really **experience** all that?

_What will it be like at home?_ Compared with Germany, my impressions of the countries will be "worked out" further and become clearer. That's how it's been every time I've changed country so far. I'll be in touch once I've collected myself.

There's one very important thing I'd like to stress once again here: without the support of my control centre back home, a lot of things would have been much, much harder. Thank you for your patience and your time! It gave me a great deal of strength. A huge, huge "thank you" for that!

Thanks also to my hosts in China, Bangkok and Singapore! It's great that we managed to meet.

And thanks too for your encouraging emails and comments! I'm looking forward to seeing you all again and I'm excited about what lies ahead.

Over the past miles I've met lots of interesting people - mostly in unbelievable situations. I've learnt a lot through you. Even if many of you will never read this blog: all the very best, from the bottom of my heart!
