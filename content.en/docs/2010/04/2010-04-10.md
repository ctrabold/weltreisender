## Saturday, 10 April 2010

Helo Malaysia!

Arrived safely in Kuala Lumpur.

My flight to the Malaysian capital leaves at 13:10 from _Suvarnabhumi_ airport. Ahead of me lie almost [50 kilometres across Bangkok](http://maps.google.de/maps?f=d&source=s_d&saddr=Soi%20Wanta%208/%E0%B8%8B%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%A2%20%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%A7%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B2%208&daddr=Route%20366/Thanon%20Lat%20Phrao/%E0%B8%96%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A5%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%9E%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%89%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A7%20to:Route%203344/Thanon%20Srinagarindra/%E0%B8%96%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A8%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B5%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%8C%20to:Thanon%20On%20Nut/%E0%B8%96%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%AD%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%B8%E0%B8%8A%20to:Flughafen%20Bangkok-Suvarnabhumi%20Nong%20Prue%20Samut%20Prakan%20Thailand&hl=de&geocode=FVOG0wAd9-79BQ;FWKk0gAdMaz-BQ;FTqZ0QAdfqn_BQ;FUxQ0QAdYff_BQ;FYrt0AAdaVUBBilLW8LggGcdMTEQMDUkOJIBDw&mra=mr&mrcr=0&via=1,2,3&dirflg=w&sll=13.785904,100.634766&sspn=0.114534,0.154324&ie=UTF8&ll=13.857497,100.53606&spn=0.014312,0.01929&z=16). No joke - and even if everything goes smoothly and I stick to the planned route, it's going to be pretty tough.

So, to be on the safe side, I set off at 6 o'clock. The ride is full of pitfalls for cyclists: overpasses that take you over junctions, shortcuts that bypass traffic lights. Plus lots of traffic, despite the early hour. I'd also like to avoid the sun, which is surprisingly hot even in the morning.

Even doing nothing, the sweat runs. A funny side effect: when crossing the road, the Thais try to use every tiny scrap of shade (walls, road signs, billboards). Often hunched over. That makes for interesting zigzag walking patterns.

Another favourite is fending off the rays with any object to hand (usually newspapers, tarpaulins or sometimes even umbrellas). Sunshine here - unlike back home - is very, very unpopular.

A small advantage: as a farang (Westerner), I often have the pavements to myself and can walk at my usual pace (weasel-quick).

Yes, the ride to the airport is very tough, but I reach my destination after a good two hours, including a short breather and without taking a single wrong turn... drenched in sweat and done in, BUT in good shape.

Just as the sun really starts to heat up, I roll into the air-conditioned halls.

The airport is surprisingly quiet and relaxed. The stress of the big city is barely noticeable. First I freshen up at leisure in the bathroom on the ground floor, change my clothes, and then make my way with Rondolf via the [travelator](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moving_walkway) to the AirAsia check-in desk (tip: avoid the lifts, they're usually packed!).

My research showed that taking a bike on AirAsia is fairly straightforward. Still, there's a small residual risk, because Bangkok airport in particular is said to have some special rules... I'm curious. Hopefully it'll all work out.

At the desk, a rather brusque lady informs me that I have to have my bike wrapped in film for 200 baht and that the bike costs another 530 baht extra. When I ask why this silly wrapping is necessary, all I get is: _don't ask, just do it!_ And with that she turns to the next customer.

I see! To get over the shock, I first go and have a proper dim sum breakfast, then simply try again. Full and smiling. :)

The dragon has vanished and this time a young man serves me _and_: everything's fine. I don't need to wrap the bike and even get free cardboard for my pedals. Everyone is very helpful and friendly. Lesson 1: food helps!

Then the usual airport routine: security check, duty-free knick-knacks, waiting for boarding. If you fancy it, you can get a last-minute foot massage for 600 baht (!) for 45 minutes (!!) (that's VERY expensive).

The moment I sit down in the boarding area, the short night and the exhausting ride catch up with me. I fall into a deep sleep. When I wake with a start, the "Boarding" sign is flashing. Plane still there. Lucky.

The plane is only half full / pleasantly empty. I have a row of seats to myself and a wonderful view of the clouds. For minutes on end I marvel at huge elephant towers, kilometre-long silk scarves of the finest ice and cute little fleecy clouds. Lovely! Then my eyes close again.

The landing in Kuala Lumpur is smooth. Immigration friendly and quick. Within minutes I've got my bike and panniers together and roll off to the food court first.

Despite the tasty food, I feel rotten. It's still a long way to Kuala Lumpur (at least 50 km) and it's slowly getting dark. I decide to take the AirAsia shuttle bus and enlarge my carbon footprint a little more for today.

Taking the bike is no problem at all. The young men handle it with a routine that surprises me. 1-2-3 the bike is on the bus and we're off. Very good!

The moment I sit down I'm asleep again. Then eyes open, closed again. And so it goes until I see the dazzling skyline of Kuala Lumpur. And then the Petronas Towers! Behind them, lightning lights up the night sky. Really impressive!

The bus stops at Kuala Lumpur's central station. From there I ride through the city. The storm has passed and it's pleasantly warm. The ride through the quiet streets of Chinatown, Little India and KLCC does me good, and I can't stop marvelling. The difference from Bangkok is huge.

As my gaze sweeps over the skyscrapers, a moped rider speaks to me. Oh no - another one of those annoying moped taxi drivers!

But as it turns out, he's a keen cyclist himself, and soon we get chatting. He introduces me to the cycling scene in KL and wants to show me his city.

He fetches his bike and we cycle together through the quiet night. Afterwards I find a pleasant hostel in the city's Golden Triangle and we say goodbye.

After so long as a couchsurfer, it's finally time for a single room (and being a loner) again. I enjoy the peace and quiet, and seconds later I'm fast asleep.

Facts on taking a bike on AirAsia from Bangkok

- The bike doesn't need to be wrapped in film or packed in a box. It should be enough to protect the pedals and other protruding and/or delicate parts.
- The bike flies as sports equipment and costs exactly ~~530~~ 520 baht extra up to 15 kilos.
- The bike can't be counted towards your free baggage allowance.
- Instead of the lifts, use the travelator / [moving walkway](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrsteig) in area E (?). The lifts are very cramped and often overcrowded.
