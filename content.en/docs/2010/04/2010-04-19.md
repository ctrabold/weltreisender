## Monday, 19 April 2010

Rumble in the Jungle - From Kuala Lipis to Kota Bharu

​17 to 19 April

Twenty-one kilometres - that's how long I'm giving this route to get interesting and exciting. After that, Malaysia can take a running jump.

In terms of impressions, it's a disturbingly empty vacuum. A bad joke, and simply not worth all the effort.

In the little village of Padang Tengku, luckily, shortly after leaving the highway I find a toilet block that offers shade and the prospect of a refreshing mini-shower.

Sadly the water's turned off. Never mind! The shade is enough for me and I'm glad to be out of the sun.

A glance at my watch shows: it's not even eleven o'clock yet!!

A few more metres and I'd have keeled over or - more likely - melted, evaporated or passed into some other (metaphysical) state of matter.

If I hadn't made a vow on the Tibetan plateau in Qinghai, China, that it can never be too hot, I'd now admit that it's too hot.

As it is, I'm simply shattered and wondering how best to get myself out of this dilemma.

At this pace I'll manage fifty kilometres today at most. Sadly, there's no town to be seen on the map. Something will turn up.

Meanwhile my thoughts are circling round a completely different idea: yesterday I read an interesting piece in the guidebook about the "Jungle Railway". It's a train that chugs leisurely through the jungle and stops at every banana leaf.

That sounds right up my street, because there's not really any jungle to be seen here - except on the map.

According to the map, the railway line should be right here. But there's no sign of any tracks anywhere.

With the biscuits almost gone, I try my luck further off the beaten track and follow the country road to the town centre.

There's a market on, and it smells gloriously of fruit and all sorts of fried things. Time for a third breakfast.

As usual, there's a building with lots of food stalls where you can eat in peace in the shade.

Whatever the time of day, there's always something going on. A few stalls are always open.
