## Wednesday, 14 April 2010

An Involuntary Sunbathe in Kuala Lumpur

​14 April

In good spirits and more or less recovered, I set off towards the east coast. Destination: Kota Bharu. Planned duration: one week.

Only the weather could still throw a spanner in the works and delay my departure, I think the evening before.

The cloudbursts in KL are fierce, and the thunder booming through the canyons of high-rises makes everything vibrate and even makes grown men in the street flinch. With every flash I count along: almost every one is a strike, and closer than "four seconds".

The only thing spoiling this natural spectacle is that, despite sheltering at a bus stop and despite my rain jacket, my clothes are soaked to the skin within five minutes. Unlikely that they'll be dry by tomorrow; likely that my whole pannier will reek of travel fug again as a result.

Not even a quick wash with soap helps: damp clothes - especially shoes! - in waterproof panniers stink within an hour at the most, and even faster in strong sunshine. Compared with that, the smell of overripe durian is probably fragrant.

There's nothing you can do about the high temperature in the panniers, except perhaps wrap up delicate things and ride in the shade as often as possible.

In China, for example, my fever thermometer gave up the ghost because of the heat in the panniers, even though it was "protected". The only reliable solution: take a digital thermometer. And ointments leak - pack them watertight!

On the plus side, I now know that even in the middle of the panniers it can get well over 44 degrees Celsius.

My measures against the stench are:

- Avoid moisture wherever possible; if rain is on the way: take shelter and wait!

- Air the panniers as often as possible

- Use every ray of sunshine for drying; pack things away again as soon as the sun disappears, because then they'll pick up moisture again!

- Strap wet things to the rear pannier if possible and let them dry in the slipstream (although I avoid this in heavy city traffic)

- Keep wet things separate from dry things, otherwise the dry ones will immediately start to stink too

- Powder shoes and socks to soak up the worst of the fumes

The fact that the next day I'm longing with every fibre of my being for a cooling shower like that from above shows just how brutally the sun works against you in the urban jungle:

Leaving the city is more of a slog than ever, and one long odyssey through blazing sun, roaring traffic and all the other big-city chaos.

The traffic routing - where there is any - is very unfamiliar, and with one-way streets and systems of parallel roads that seem utterly senseless to me and then suddenly lead in different directions, it's simply frustrating. Spontaneous changes of lane or direction are blocked by massive concrete walls, so you often have to make detours of several kilometres to the next U-turn (riding against the traffic is often far too dangerous because there's so little space).

The upshot: it takes me four hours to find my way out of the city, completely drained.

Via the six-lane highway I finally reach the country road to Kota Bharu, just before the toll station.

All of a sudden it's quieter and I can finally catch my breath... for about a hundred metres, until the next village, where a group of young machos greets me straight away and stages comical races around me on mopeds and bicycles.

Once all the other villagers have been informed of my arrival, they leave me alone again and I work my way on up the hill, concentrating hard.

After all that effort, the day thankfully ends fairly relaxed: by chance I find a little campsite where I can hang up my mosquito net in a hut.

I sit with the owner until late into the night, eating delicious noodles and chatting about Malaysia and the rest of the world.

For dessert he puts on "Die Hard 4" for me and disappears to join his wife at the next table. I'm dead tired, but don't want to be rude, so I pretend to enjoy the film. My eyes close immediately.

So all I catch of the mighty Bruce is, at most, the din in my half-sleep. The "story" will probably remain a mystery to me forever...

When the credits roll, I quickly sneak off to my hut. That's that over with too.

It can only get better, I think, and listen to the chirping and whistling in the forest until I fall asleep.

PS: If you have trouble imagining what a ride like this feels like, I "recommend" a journey of the imagination (!) in rush-hour traffic on the A5 motorway from Frankfurt to Langen, Germany: start at Konstablerwache, work your way past the trade fair grounds to the Frankfurt-West interchange, then onto the A5 straight on to the exit for the B486. Follow the country road and roll casually down to the station.

If you like, you can also picture yourself wearing a clown costume and do an extra lap of Griesheim. :)
