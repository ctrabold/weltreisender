## Thursday, 1 April 2010

Ko Tao – From Wow! to Ouch! in Seven Days

My first impression of "[Turtle Island](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Tao)" is overwhelming:

From my bungalow I have a glorious view over Tanote Bay, waves lap gently in the background, birds are singing everywhere and the sky is a brilliant blue. Wow!

Unlike Koh Chang, it's also cleaner and much quieter here. And there's a German dive centreD Calypso Diving Kohtao! So the main activity for the coming days is settled: diving, diving, diving!

A few minutes after checking in I'm racing the sea creatures on the house reef, snorkelling through the soft coral gardens and marvelling at the underwater world. In places the water is as warm as a bath, so you can stay in extra long, until your skin is nice and wrinkly. :)

On the second day it's into the water with full scuba gear. The first dive is the so-called _check dive_, where the most important skills are checked and, if necessary, refreshed.

Everything goes perfectly smoothly for my dive buddy and me, and the exploration of the house reef that follows is by far the most relaxing and at the same time most interesting dive I've ever done.

It makes a huge difference whether you snorkel down to the bottom and then have to come back up after a short while (often just when it's getting most interesting), or whether you can hover calmly and watch the enchanting goings-on underwater - for example the gorgeous [Christmas tree worms](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihnachtsbaumwurm). Also lovely: the stately progress of the [sea cucumbers](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seegurke).

The pace of the dive is exactly to my taste. Werner leads us calmly through the water and shows us lots of interesting little creatures (very funny: a swaying [long-nosed shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caridina_gracilirostris) on a [whip coral / Leptogorgia virgulata)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptogorgia_virgulata).

The maximum depth is 15 metres, which often means the full colour spectrum and plenty of air. Even so, the dives are always too short...

All in all I do nine dives in five days - until my right jaw hurts after a dive and my right ear suddenly "closes up" - I can practically hear nothing. Ouch!

For three days I can hardly sleep for the pain. A visit to the doctor brings some relief: the eardrum is intact but red. Painkillers help me get a few hours' sleep.

According to the doctor, the pain should ease off quickly over the next few days, but instead it gets rapidly worse and becomes truly unbearable. I've never had pain like it in my head.

So I head back to Bangkok earlier than planned and go straight to the ear clinic. There I get antibiotics and stronger painkillers. Result: the pain is gone within a few hours, but my hearing stays the same.

It seems to be an inflammation of the jaw joint, properly fired up by working the unfamiliar mouthpiece, combined with equalising and seawater in the sinuses. As a result the fluid can't drain away, which is what's affecting my hearing. It also feels as if there's constantly water in my ear.

The verdict after another hospital visit: no diving for at least five weeks, bed rest and only easy-to-chew food - NO meat. Once the fluid can drain, my hearing should be fully restored.

Well, these are tough days, I can tell you, but I'm trying to take it on the chin. It's not as if I'd been looking forward to diving here for over ten months... :-(

Anticipation really is the greatest pleasure, I suppose. And health is the most precious thing of all.

Wishing you all a lovely, healthy Easter!
