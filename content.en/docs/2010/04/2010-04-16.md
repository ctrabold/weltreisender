## Friday, 16 April 2010

A Retching Vomit Parade – Venting My Frustration from Raub to Kuala Lipis

​16 April

7:30 - the perfect time for a good old spew under my window!

Before the fumes even rise up to me, I've packed my panniers in my half-sleep. As my nose starts to tingle, thank God, I can close the door behind me and leave the hotel.

What a dreadful night this is coming to an end: the air conditioning rattles away at full blast and yet the room stays muggy, hot and stuffy. The only ones who really feel at home in the sticky air are the mosquitoes, buzzing merrily around my head.

Under the canopy two birds are mating, squealing loudly; in the distance, after a long struggle, a cat finally coughs up a hairball.

Can it get any worse? Yes! Every few minutes, cars with broken exhausts roar along the street, trying to outdo each other.

That seems to be a national sport here. At any rate, I've never heard so many engines rattle so loudly and pathetically in any other country.

The deep, jagged, utterly ugly gurgling of the discontented engines booms through the night and wakes me up every time.

On the spot I rename the country "Could you turn it down, please?!".

As luck would have it, Mr Vomit is staying in my hotel and is having another go in the toilet on my floor just as I flee downstairs to the exit.

It's the first time I've seriously put on my face mask from China to protect myself from infection. Oh, maybe also to protect myself from just about everything here.

I'm massively relieved to leave the town and, tired, disappointed and seething with anger, I do the first twenty kilometres at a forced march.

I think the last time I felt like this was in Kyrgyzstan, at Song Köl lake, after the night in the yurt.

When a sign points to country road C144, I gather all my courage and hope, leave the hectic expressway and take the turning onto the country road.

The difference is overwhelming and instantly noticeable. No word of a lie: the people, the landscape - I myself am suddenly completely transformed. I positively thaw out, and see nature and friendly people again!

By the first hill at the latest, everything is hunky-dory again and I sweat all the frustration out of my system. There's no energy left over for grumpiness.

Just before the descent into the valley I meet the lorry drivers again who'd overtaken me earlier. With all sorts of faces and wisecracks they cheer me on and even make me laugh again. They also warn me about the roadworks lurking further down on a sharp bend. Thanks for that!

The moral of the story: cyclist, seek your long road well away from the main roads!

In the valley, as well as some cheerful road workers, the humidity is sadly waiting for me again.

It's remarkable how directly your performance drops. It's incredibly exhausting, and the "performance drop" is quite comparable to the one at high altitude: despite giving it everything, you hardly get anywhere.

I set myself smaller goals, stop as often as possible, cool down in air-conditioned petrol station shops and drink vast amounts of tea, water, juice and isotonic drinks.

That leaves only a narrow window for cycling:

From 11 o'clock it's really already too hot. If possible, I then look for a restaurant where I can bridge the time until 5 pm.

After that I ride on at a deliberately easy pace until I reach my destination.

Today too. At a roadside restaurant I watch the World Cup qualifier (?) between Denmark and Portugal. Lovely to see how the Malays get caught up in it and celebrate the goals!

Afterwards there's a documentary about the host venues in Africa. Man, am I looking forward to the World Cup!

Refreshed and fortified, I set off on the last kilometres towards Kuala Lipis.

There I quickly find a hotel with a hot shower, air con, WiFi and a lovely night market nearby.

It's quiet, and I have hopes of a restful night's sleep – WITHOUT any spewterruptions ;)
