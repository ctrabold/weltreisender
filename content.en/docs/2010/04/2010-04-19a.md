## Monday, 19 April 2010

Rumble in the Jungle – From Kuala Lipis to Kota Bharu Part 2

17 to 19 April

My third breakfast is chicken curry with rice and lots of sauce.

In Malaysia, getting food often works like this:

In a building rather like a waiting hall there are several outlets (kiosk, food stall/small restaurant). All neatly numbered and clean. A kind of village canteen. You sit on plastic chairs at long tables. It's fairly functional, but cosy all the same (or because of it?).

The most important thing of all, anyway, is that you can eat in the shade and the fans create a light breeze. Quiet is less important. Decor completely irrelevant. With my food I drink "teh tarik". That's black tea with lots of condensed milk. You can have it hot or with ice cubes. Always delicious.

As I sit there sipping my tea, the whole hall suddenly vibrates and, after a fair bit of din, there's a thud and a train stops a few metres away, right in front of my eyes.

The station I was looking for is right in front of me (it just looks different from how I'd imagined it). At this point I'd like to stress once again that the "drink tea and wait" method has proved itself surprisingly often, especially in moments of utter despair!

Meanwhile my decision has firmed up: I'll ride back to Kuala Lipis and catch the train to Kota Bharu from there.

The ride back shows me I've made the right choice: for one thing, a fierce thunderstorm breaks behind me (in my original target area) and almost catches me up. For another, my legs are empty and my head full of sun-fug. I'm hugely relieved when I check into the hotel on familiar ground and can lie down in my air-conditioned room.

The next lunchtime everything works out beautifully: taking the bike is no problem at all. The conductor is positively delighted to lend a hand, and the train leaves on time and lives up to its name. For the equivalent of five euros I ride leisurely through deep green jungle. Often there's barely a hand's breadth between the train and the GREEN!

The train stops every few kilometres, people get off or on, disappear or emerge from the bush somewhere. A great experience.

As a pleasant side effect, I even get a real (!) urge to ride the route by bike (some day). My explorer's instinct is awakened, and for that reason alone I'm absolutely delighted to have chosen the train over the dreary highway.

I reach Kota Bharu in top form and find a room in a very nice guesthouse. My host explains where the best place to buy durian is. And at the night market I get a great welcome present: a "Special-National-Double-Deluxe-Burger".

A striking number of street stalls here sell burgers for next to nothing: 70 cents for the super-duper version – brilliant!

Over the next few days I'm heading for the Perhentian Islands. There are no roads or cars there. Just beach, jungle and sea.

I'm a bit excited (as we say in Hessian dialect, "ebbes uffjerescht") and hope it'll be nicer than in Thailand.
