## Saturday, 10 April 2010

Paradise Uncovered

Anyone who catches themselves calling my pictures of Ko Tao or Ko Chang heavenly, dreamy or idyllic - or who even feels a pang of envy - may find some relief in my report:

It's _not_ as great as it looks!

The beach and the restaurants are swarming with midges and mosquitoes. Even first thing in the morning I have to get out the chemical weapons and slather myself from head to toe.

Because the water often comes from a central reservoir, illnesses such as outbreaks of vomiting and diarrhoea can spread faster than on the mainland. Poor drinking water quality also means poorer hygiene when washing up and showering. On Ko Tao especially, I smelt far worse after a shower than before.

The beaches are littered with beer bottles, plastic rubbish, and lumps of oil and tar. I also saw lots of rubbish heaps inland. So you're constantly reminded what kind of civilisation you live in - no matter how far you are from the mainland or how small and remote the island may be.

The food is often overpriced, which is understandable given it's an island. After all, everything has to be shipped in from far away. But in the long run the food is boring and can't match city food for quality. I tried all sorts of island food, including local "specialities", but by the fourth restaurant at the latest the taste experience was repeating itself. It was _good food_, no question, but feasts like on the mainland were nowhere to be found.

The people are... different. Tourists are either arrogant, aggressive and inconsiderate _(I want the total island experience now! ... and cows!! ... and this and that!)_, or already so frustrated or _isolated_ that they - usually unasked - talk other people's ears off with some nonsense or other, in the hope of finally getting a bit of variety in their day.

Admiring the full moon at the beach bar to gentle music and lapping waves is romantic. Having to put up with the endless - usually English - waffle at the next table about some abstruse world view or alternative life strategy, on the other hand, is very *un*romantic and annoying.

There are pleasant people to talk to as well, of course - mostly Germans! But beware of the half-time Full Moon Party ravers shuffling slowly past wrapped in silk scarves, the beach nymphs, or the older gentlemen with MacBooks who sit in the same spot every day just waiting to recruit newcomers for their next monologue!

The locals - wherever you are on the island - are fixated on tourism. In town you hear the question "Where you go my friend? Need taxi?" at every turn. Locals in the island's interior are obviously - and perhaps rightly - annoyed to see tourists in their own retreat: "Now they're coming here too...". (The locals at each resort are, of course, very friendly!)

Incidentally, I noticed a similar phenomenon of aversion when tourist met tourist. Whenever tourists ran into each other far off the main tourist trails, the first (instinctive) reaction was a mixture of "damn, what's he doing here?" and "just don't see or be seen." Then, at the meeting on the narrow footpath that couldn't be avoided: brief eye contact, a nod and maybe a quick "Hello!".

These are just a few examples from my island life so far.

My conclusion: after five days of island life at the most, I start going stir-crazy. Apparently I'm more of a city person than I thought. At any rate, _not_ an island person. On an island I get claustrophobic faster than at the busiest night market or in the biggest shopping centre.

In my opinion, island life becomes bleak far too quickly, and I can only advise everyone not to fall for the illusions in travel agents' brochures, let alone photos on the internet. Tip one: test your chosen "dream island" for just a few days first!

Tip two: definitely plan some activities to balance things out, such as diving. Then you can stand it for longer. :)

The experience on which this text is based rests on the strictly analytically gathered, purely subjective impressions of my visits to the Thai islands of Ko Chang and Ko Tao. Changes of opinion due to further island visits in Malaysia reserved.
