## Wednesday, 10 February 2010

Trat

From _Ban Khlong Yai_ I roll leisurely on to [Trat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trat). The hills disappear and it flattens out.

Around midday I reach the town, a little sweaty, and at first I'm somewhat shocked: half-crumbling concrete blocks line the street and there's hardly anything that makes you want to linger. I quickly find an internet café where I can decide in peace what to do next. It's too loud and hot out on the street. _Should I carry on to Chanthaburi or look for a guesthouse here?_

I decide to explore the town a bit first, maybe grab a bite to eat, and take it from there.

A few metres further on I turn into a side street and all of a sudden the traffic falls silent, children are playing in the street and gentle music drifts into my ears from somewhere. In an instant, a calm, relaxed landscape of cafés and bars opens up in front of me.

Amazing! That's how quickly it happens. The town is unrecognisable! I stop to take it all in, and a friendly lady is already asking whether I'm looking for a room. Two minutes later I'm moving into it.

While I'm uploading my pictures on a fast, modern computer, André from Germany starts chatting to me. The pictures can wait - the beer and above all the stories can't! So we sit for ages in the guesthouse's cool, wonderfully cosy beer garden, chatting about travelling. He's come from Vietnam and rode the same route through Cambodia as I did. What a coincidence! We must have missed each other by a day.

In the evening we go to the night market together. It's pleasantly warm and I take to the market straight away. It's organised chaos, people bustling about everywhere, and there's so much different food! It's clean, not too loud. Just right. A dream!

We have noodle soup and fried chicken. For dessert there are fresh fruit shakes and sweet rice balls.

Properly full for once, I fall into bed and sleep brilliantly.
