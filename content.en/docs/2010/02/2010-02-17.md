## Wednesday, 17 February 2010

A Short Break at Long Beach

​12 to 16 February

At midday on 12 February I take the ferry across to the island of [Koh Chang](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koh_Chang). Once there, I head for the east coast. The west coast, with all its hotels and discos, strikes me as very dodgy. Besides, the day after tomorrow is Chinese New Year and, according to my guesthouse landlady, all hell will break loose - the hotels are probably all fully booked.

Cheerfully confident of finding somewhere cosy to stay on the east coast all the same, I pedal up the first hills towards the southern tip. There's even a diving flag marked on the map there. Maybe I'll finally get a dive in!

The road is superbly surfaced but very hilly. It's a little muggier than on the mainland, but green and interesting all the way.

All of a sudden the road turns into a narrow lane through a little village. I get closer to the locals and can often see straight into the open wooden huts. Lots of children and dogs are playing in the street and there's music in the air. _Proper Thailand_, I think to myself - when, like a hammer blow, a sign looms up in front of me: _Thai-German: Unterkunft saubillig!_ (German for "dirt-cheap rooms!") I can hardly believe my eyes and have to get off the bike, I'm laughing so hard.

A Thai woman comes out of the house and looks at me, baffled. Then we both laugh.

Shortly afterwards I'm sitting under the parasol pouring myself a nice dark wheat beer. Heaven.

Sadly, the room is neither dirt-cheap nor quiet. The dogs bark outside all night, the mosquito net has more holes than a fishing net, and at 6 in the morning the village's fireworks master sets off the first mat of bangers - right outside my window. I'm bolt upright in bed and wide awake. Happy New Year!

A hearty breakfast makes up a little for the ordeal, but I still leave this sleepy spot and ride back a little way. At the next junction I turn off for _Long Beach_. Among other things there's a monument there, which I've picked as the goal for today's stage.

My plan is flattened by the brutal climbs that tower up in front of me about two kilometres further on. I didn't see anything like this even on the crazy mountain tracks in Kyrgyzstan (at least not as often :)). The "road" goes steeply up and then straight back down. Then it all repeats a few times. The bends are very tight and the surface - where there is one - extremely crumbly.

I drag myself up the first climb, have to stop for breath halfway, and then ride straight into the arms of a man who's waiting at the top with a broad grin. With welcoming gestures he signals that I should lift my bike onto the back of his pick-up. He'll drive me to the beach, where he also has rooms free... if I'd like.

At first I protest - somehow this is all happening too fast, and I've got time! But then his compelling arguments win me over. He doesn't want any money for the lift, and once again shows me very vividly how bad the road gets. I can always torture myself on the way back if I'm that keen.

Just as well I swallowed my pride (and my fear of the unknown), because the route is more than adventurous.

Completely covered in dust and shaken to bits, I arrive at his bungalow resort five spectacular kilometres later. The price for the hut is fair, and after thanking him again I retreat, only to launch a targeted counter-attack on the menu shortly afterwards.

The following days fly by. My daily routine is strictly limited to

- getting up
- breakfast
- strolling to the beach
- swimming
- lunch
- swimming
- lunch
- drying off
- watching the sunset
- dinner
- sleeping

At first I find this routine utterly abhorrent and have real (!) trouble with "doing nothing". A little exploratory ride on the bike only heightens my fears: apart from beach, sea and jungle there's NOTHING else to see here... the monument is - well - boring. Me, hemmed in by jagged mountains, far from civilisation... panic or chill out? I accept my situation, and by the second day I'm feeling muuuuch better.

On the beach there's a fantastic Thai massage under the palm trees, with natural meditation music: gentle waves and birdsong accompany my torture. It's incredible what they do to you. Picture it: _OK... she's now standing on my thigh!?_ The massage goes deep and relaxes me just right. The next day I'm slightly stiff.

Over evening drinks I meet interesting people from all over the world. My bike is, and remains, an attraction. They've never seen anything like it here (most people arrive by speedboat, scooter or shared taxi).

Still: after almost a week I've had enough. Besides, I'm meeting Ralph in Bangkok at the weekend. Time to get my skates on. Tomorrow it's back to the mainland!
