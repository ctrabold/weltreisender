## Tuesday, 9 February 2010

Bye bye Cambodia! Sawaadi Krap Thailand!

On 9 February I ride into Thailand at a leisurely pace. Everything goes smoothly at the border.

As I got my Thai visa in Kunming, I'm given permission to stay until mid-April without any questions.

The road number changes from 48 to 318. Otherwise little changes. It stays gently hilly and the traffic only picks up slightly.

Just under 20 kilometres further on I reach the first sizeable town, _Ban Khlong Yai_, where, after a long search and some baffling communication games, I finally get a room:

**Me:** Do you have a free room?
**Her** (scowling): NO!
then walks off outside without another word
**Me:** Oh well…
as I get back on the bike, I see her waving me over to a room.
**Me:** There we go!
I pay and retreat to my room

So my first impression is fairly underwhelming:

1. Nobody speaks English (I'd have expected otherwise, especially in touristy Thailand)
2. Road signs only in Thai. Until now, everything in Asia has always been bilingual (but I knew that beforehand!).
3. Poor on the internet front. Super-restrictive and OUTDATED internet cafés (IE6, no German keyboard, no USB access). HELP!

I really don't expect anything special from Thailand and go in as open-minded as I can. Let's see what happens.

But of course the people are nice on the whole, and interested too (although somehow I warmed to the Cambodians more).

The landscape is pretty boring. Yes, there's the sea, and yes, there are palm trees, BUT that's just nothing special any more - unlike the Crimea, for example, where every bend made me go WOW.

In short: I'M JUST FEELING A BIT FALANG! (see [http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falang](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falang), the Thai word for Westerners)
