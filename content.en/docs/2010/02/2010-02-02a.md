## Tuesday, 2 February 2010

Eggs, Eggs, Eggs

​2 February 2010

I've never felt so disgusted and wretched before, during and after a meal:

Happy and brimming with impressions from Angkor, I arrive back in Phnom Penh.

The bus stops near the riverfront. The guesthouse is too far away for my liking, so I look for somewhere to stay nearby.

While I'm searching, I'm intercepted by Manfred. He runs a restaurant in Phnom Penh and invites me to join him at his table.

I acknowledge my usual scepticism about invitations like this, but I can't see any risk and sit down with Christian and Willi. The potato salad with Nuremberg sausages is delicious and the conversation flows.

Afterwards my namesake Christian shows me his favourite pub round the corner. The atmosphere is pleasant and we get chatting for hours, until shortly before midnight a ravenous hunger takes hold of me.

I search the street in desperation for a food stall. Nothing! Everyone is either packing up or long gone. On the spot I wish I were back in China.

With a bit of luck, I do manage to get hold of a hard-boiled egg. The waitress at the pub bursts out laughing when she sees me with it and asks, with a strange look on her face, whether I really like that.

I say: of course! I like eggs, and I thank her for the plate, salt and pepper.

They all stare at me expectantly. I start to feel a bit unsure and ask what the deal is with the egg.

She explains that I'm holding a Cambodian speciality: a half-hatched duck egg.

My appetite changes in an instant. I feel my stomach tighten. The last time I saw something like this was in my biology degree. Eating it is unthinkable!

Suddenly I don't fancy it any more. My hunger has vanished - it was never there - and, embarrassed, I try to give the egg to the waitress.

Offended, she snaps at me - jokingly, but firmly - and in the end cracks the egg open for me without batting an eyelid.

She smashes the shell with a knife and brown liquid seeps out. Then a little lump plops onto the plate. Revolting. You can see everything! There's no way I can eat that!

Everything in me rebels, and I flatly refuse to honour my pledge to try _everything_ that's put in front of me at least _once_. Forget it! Nobody would notice anyway.

In the end, though, curiosity - or rather peer pressure - wins out. Tentatively I cut the lump into rough pieces and separate the head-like bit from the rest. Anything that looks off-putting stays on the plate. The rest gets swallowed.

It's just like the first time I climbed the ten-metre diving board. Going up is fairly easy, but then - at the latest when you look over the edge of the platform down at the water - the jitters set in. Your legs turn to jelly and every eye is on the diver. Climbing back down is THE WORST! And so the first teeny-tiny morsel does end up on the spoon and - after one last inspection - finally in my mouth.

Tastes and smells like... a normal egg, if it weren't for those little splinters (bones). The beak? The toes? Oh God! I'm about to throw up into my food. Banish those thoughts!

At some point I've tried enough. The worst part: now I feel sick AND I'm even hungrier!

When the pub closes, we retreat to the hotel, where we sit on the balcony until sunrise, enjoying the peace and quiet.
