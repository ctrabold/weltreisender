## Monday, 25 January 2010

Yuck, Spiders! Nooo, Yum!

Instead of the expected arrival in Phnom Penh, at 7:30 pm there's a longer bus stop to grab some food.

In fact, since the last break in Kratie my appetite has been steadily improving.

So I reckon it was a classic case of sunstroke, which got into my head despite my sun hat, lots of water and cooling off in the river (the food at the homestay didn't agree with me either, though).

Be that as it may: the ride in the air-conditioned bus does me good, and along the way I get to know the man sitting next to me, Stephan from Argentina.

Together we try the deep-fried grasshoppers and tarantulas being touted at the night market.

Eating instructions: Stephan recommends removing the grasshoppers' lower hind legs, because they have little barbs that like to get stuck between your teeth. The locals happily pop them in whole, though.

Tarantulas go straight into the mouth whole - and chew fast (!) before they try to crawl back out!! :D

The grasshoppers are a bit too oily and bland for me, but tasty all the same. Somewhere along the lines of bacon crisps. Very crunchy.

The tarantulas are more substantial and more "to my taste". Rather like a good piece of soft smoked ham.

For dessert I buy a gloriously refreshing mango (mango season is just starting here, and you can get these little treats for next to nothing on every street corner).

After this culinary interlude we carry on. Only 75 kilometres to the capital!

The bus driver now tends to communicate with other road users by flashing his headlights rather than honking. When I'm not spellbound by the light show on the road, which I can't make head or tail of (lights on, lights off, full beam), I can even catch forty winks.

We carry on through fairly dreary countryside, and I'm glad to watch it rush past from the bus.
