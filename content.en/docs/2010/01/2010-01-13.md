## Wednesday, 13 January 2010

Luang Namtha by Night

The horror stories from Laos just keep coming:

On a night-time stroll through the alleys I almost get my legs trapped when the pavements are unexpectedly rolled up just before ten, and on my subsequent visit to the night market I fall into a deep, yawning void that I can only free myself from with great effort.

In short: I feel fantastic here and am enjoying the Laotians' more relaxed pace to the full.

Still: even though lots of travellers "warned" me, the contrast with chaotic China is really stark.

If I sniff or eat slightly noisily, I feel shabby - foreign, even.

Here people eat with cutlery - chopsticks are only for particular dishes - and after the meal the table usually looks cleaner than before (no Chinese battlefield of snot and spat-out bones).

And as a rule people speak muuuch more quietly. So far there's been no sign of musical assault by techno rubbish either.

Those are my first impressions of Laos, 80 kilometres beyond the Chinese border, from the provincial capital Luang Namtha.
