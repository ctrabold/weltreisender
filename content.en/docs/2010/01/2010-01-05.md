## Tuesday, 5 January 2010

Bus Blues

​5 January 2010

The train arrives in Kunming on time on Monday at midday.

I quickly head to the youth hostel and pack my things. Tomorrow I say goodbye to Kunming and take the night bus to Jinghong! I'm really looking forward to it.

When I arrive at the "Long Distance Bus Station" the next morning, I'm turned away at the counter, short and sharp. _Mei you_ - there's no bus to Jinghong! I protest, and after endless back and forth I get a number and various Chinese characters scribbled on a sheet of paper.

Crestfallen, I leave the waiting hall. MAN! All that "bus-optimised" packing for nothing (3 instead of 6 bags)!

As I repack my panniers outside the hall, the usual crowd gathers around me. Amid the eternal Chinese murmuring about my map, tyres, panniers and so on, my weary ears suddenly hear a friendly woman's voice asking: "May I help you?!" - because it's so rare, once more: "May I help you?!"

YES! I explain my predicament and get some more key facts:

1. Buses towards Laos now leave from the South Station. It's miles and miles from the railway station and isn't even on my (outdated) map.
2. The note gives the number of the bus that will take me to this new station. A pretty useless piece of information, since I'm going there by bike - or so it seems.

I follow her "directions" and end up stranded at the main railway station. For the life of me I can't find any other way from here. In situations like this you feel pretty stupid.

So I carry on asking my way with hands and feet, get an up-to-date city map of Kunming from a policeman and ride back with it to my English-speaking angel.

No sooner am I back at the bus station than I see two big, help-seeking eyes heading towards me: Ben from Canada. He arrived from Beijing a few minutes ago, has hands cracked from the icy cold there, and - just like me shortly before - is wandering around the overcrowded bus station looking for someone who speaks English!

I fill him in on the situation here and then take him to the bus angel. Thanks to the most rudimentary English, we get a good deal further and receive all the information we need. How nice when people understand each other!

Before I set off on the long trek to the bus station, we grab a bite to eat and chat about travelling and cycling. Ben is planning a longer bike tour too and is very interested.

A few tips later, I set off into the unknown.

The road goes past the airport, through an industrial estate, then come motorway bridges and another industrial estate. I almost give up hope and turn back. Despite the map, I've lost my bearings. Nothing constructive can possibly come of this!

Through the haze of exhaust fumes I make out a bus stop a few metres ahead: 154. So I'm on the right track, and I simply keep going until I come out somewhere...

The plan works. Suddenly the surroundings change again, big exhibition halls (?) appear and the roads get wider. And there on the left I actually see coaches parked!

Twenty minutes later I'm on the sleeper bus to Jinghong. Rondolf is safely stowed in the belly of the bus, and we leave on time.

What a relief! And now, quick quick, off to the south!
