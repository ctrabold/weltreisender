## Friday, 15 January 2010

Robbie Williams and Back

I'm planning two day stages from Luang Namtha to Huay Xai.

On day one it's an easy-going 60 kilometres or so through gentle hills to Vieng Phoukha. I check into a lovely guesthouse there at around 5 pm, nicely sweaty, and enjoy the evening atmosphere on my wooden terrace with the window open. From the hill there's a great view over the village.

The next day starts with a magnificent breakfast: 4 fried eggs with bacon, 4 slices of bread and jam, and lots of tea.

Highly motivated, I set off on the next stage: today it's over 120 kilometres through the mountains. The first climb starts after 20 kilometres and gains a good 400 metres. Then, at kilometres 60 and 80, come several waves with two impressive peaks of another 200 to 300 metres each.

The route profile looks impressive enough on the map. In practice it's even more impressive, because it's constantly up and down, and the little hills in between add up too.

In every village I'm given a frenzied welcome and briefly feel as big as a rock star. How lovely!

I usually ride slowly through the villages, because they're either at the bottom of a valley or on top of a hill.

Long before I arrive, "Sabaidee!" rings out from everywhere. With the sun beating down, at some point all that reaches my mushy head is a wall of noise. After a while the cheering gets too much for me again. Some children want to stop me - in my state a veeery bad idea. At some point it does start to grate. Still, I try to smile or at least wave as often as possible, even though I feel absolutely wretched. After a while I hardly notice my dry throat any more. Everything burns. And then the road surface changes from OK to very bad.

At kilometre 65, ambition / madness gets the better of me and I start a little race up the mountain with a Thai lorry creeping up behind me. I win the race, but after that I lose all along the line. My legs get heavier and heavier, and on the last hill I push.

Even though I'm taking baby steps, my head pounds more with every step. My whole head is nothing but veins - no sign of a brain - and all I can think is: what if a little vein bursts up there now? Who'll clean up the mess?

The road keeps spiralling round the mountain. Just before the top I pull off to the left onto a small patch of field and collapse into the grass from exhaustion.

Even the usually so effective injection of Sprite doesn't help. It's too much. I take a longer break and try to enjoy the view to the romantic chirping of the crickets. I'll probably reach my destination in the dark again.

I no longer know how long I sit there. A few moped riders go by and cheer me on. Wow, that does me good! And suddenly I'm rolling again, until at last it goes downhill.

On the descent the endorphins catch up with me and I let out a primal scream. Arms raised high, I race down the slope. I haven't experienced anything this hard since Kyrgyzstan!

The road is great again. A few hidden bumps make the descent risky too, though. A totally battered lorry in the ditch on a half-tight bend brings me back down to earth with a bump. It looks bad.

In the next village I see two backpackers sitting by the roadside. I stop and have a little chat. They're from Italy and waiting for the bus, which is due any moment. And so it is: as I turn round, the pick-up bus is already pulling in.

The ride costs the equivalent of 80 cents. On the spur of the moment I jump on and ride the last 40 kilometres in the back with Rondolf (the other two sit up front with the driver).

The warm evening air does me good, and I watch rural life go by, fascinated. My tunnel vision had sadly hidden these lovely sights from me. Now and then cramp in my legs wakes me from my daydreams, and I try to stretch as best I can in the cramped space. Otherwise it's just great fun riding towards the sun like this.

Happy and very relieved, I arrive in Huay Xai. It's still light, and a little way from the tourist centre I find a good guesthouse. The owner recommends his favourite Laotian restaurant, where I top up my electrolytes with a very tasty soup.

Tomorrow I'll take a rest day and sort out the onward journey to Luang Prabang.
