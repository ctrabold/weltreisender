## Saturday, 13 March 2010

One Night in Bangkok – as if!

My stay in the Thai capital is now entering its fourth week. Crazy! And an unexpected turn in my journey, even for me.

_But why?_ Hard to describe. The city itself isn't actually all that overwhelming. It's chaotic, dirty, loud. Disturbing?

At the same time relaxing, interesting, lively. Inspiring!

Ever since I arrived I've been puzzling over why the city grabs me so much. Maybe it's the contrast of all the impressions. I don't know.

In no other city have I got so completely lost, felt the adrenaline in the traffic so strongly and had to fight for my place.

Never in my wildest dreams would I, in any other city, have sat down so naturally in a street bar and marvelled at the hustle and bustle right under my nose. Until dawn. Without the slightest fear that anyone would harm a hair on my head.

And I've had more fun than I've had in months. All without any bar-fine nonsense or constantly buying drinks for girls.

In no other city have I smiled so much and so gladly, made [wais](http://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wai_%28Gru%C3%9F%29) (the Thai greeting), felt such body language and warmth, as here.

Even if it isn't the deepest, most honest smile - it's a smile, and it does you good.

At the same time, I've never engaged with a country as intensely as with Thailand. Even though I arrived with as few expectations as possible, the wishful pictures and dreamy notions are just there, and they've been thoroughly reshaped or washed away.

Here I got through the deepest emotional low in months, and just a few hours later met wonderful people who have deeply enriched and shaped my journey.

On 18 February I take the bus from Trat to Bangkok. Cyclists I meet in Trat strongly advise me against riding there. They say it's uninteresting and extremely unpleasant because of the heavy traffic. Besides, I've got a date with Ralph on Friday and have no choice but to take the bus as a "lift".

My first contact with Bangkok is the ride from the bus terminal far in the north to the hotel by the river far in the south. What makes this venture particularly exciting is that my city map of Bangkok only shows the very centre (as I find out later). So I follow my instincts and my compass.

As so often when cycling through a city, I already see many of the sights and pick up some unique impressions - besides the gigantic traffic, I get to experience the police at work.

The road is closed in every direction for almost half an hour for some important member of the government, then the VIP car drives onto the road with what sounds to me like a very funny horn melody, and quickly disappears round the first bend. Then everyone roars off again.

After almost 20 sweaty city kilometres I arrive at the River View Guesthouse.

That's where I spend the first weekend: during the day mostly by the river and in Chinatown. In the evenings Ralph and I go out around Sukhumvit / Asoke Station. The nightlife is colourful, new, a touch too frenetic.

After more than nine months on the road, I'm finding it noticeably hard to throw myself into the noisy, artificial goings-on.

In the second week I move to a hotel at Asoke Station, to be closer to all this action. Although constantly "getting lost" in Chinatown introduces me to some very interesting corners of Bangkok, commuting to Sukhumvit takes too much time. Even so, it was lovely to feel the Chinese bustle I've grown so fond of once again.

The new hotel is very comfortably fitted out: as well as massage, there's a sun terrace with a pool, a gym and even a sauna. Exactly: befitting my station.

Working out above the rooftops of Bangkok feels incredibly good. The warm wind plays gently around me as I do my laps on the cross-trainer. Leg work without any traffic noise for once.

After the workout it's into the sauna, heated specially for me, where this time the _Aufguss_ (the German sauna ritual of pouring water on the coals) is performed by the best sauna master in Asia in person: me! Nobody else is anywhere near the sauna, so I can go wild without any embarrassment.

A sauna in Bangkok - is that, er, really necessary? All I can say is that I feel much better after the sauna sessions and the sweat flows far less uncontrollably. Plus, the essential oils visibly clear the airways of all sorts of grime accumulated over the past weeks.

80 degrees Celsius at almost 60% humidity is fun anywhere in the world. :)

Anyway: after the sauna, my craving for a double-triple litre of shandy is even greater than usual. So after the torments of hell it's off for a treat at the German-run restaurant "Otto" round the corner. There I enjoy fine potato salad and draught Paulaner.

Afterwards I stroll along Soi Cowboy and have a nightcap at the _Easy Bar_ on the street, before tumbling back into the hotel lobby, where the young man from reception drags me excitedly in front of the TV: Everyone! Everyone! Football!

Ah! Champions League! While I munch on my grasshoppers, VfB Stuttgart are playing FC Barcelona on the huge screen. Nice to watch (at least the first half - after that I go up to my room and fall asleep straight away)!

The room is quiet and at last I have time to sort through my impressions and collect myself.

My decision is as follows: at the end of the second week I'll buy a train ticket to Surat Thani (southern Thailand), and from there I'll cycle on to the Malaysian border and beyond.

---

Two years later the Easy Bar has completely changed, and I get relieved of 800 baht. Every moment is unique.
